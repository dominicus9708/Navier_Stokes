# Translation completeness and nonlinear coupling bridge

Date: 2026-08-12

Status: **BRIDGE DESIGN + COMPUTATIONAL CHECK + OPEN PROOF OBLIGATION**.

## 1. Why one fixed center is insufficient

The celestial-sphere viewpoint uses observation spheres centered at an analysis point `x_0`:

\[
S_r(x_0)=\{x\in\mathbb R^3:|x-x_0|=r\}.
\]

A fixed origin is convenient for a benchmark, but it is not translation complete. A proof quantity intended for arbitrary Navier–Stokes data must either be translation invariant by construction or take a supremum/integral over all relevant centers.

For the translated/rescaled Gaussian double-curl benchmark

\[
u_{c,\lambda,A}(x)=A\lambda u_0(\lambda(x-c)),
\]

with

\[
c=(1.5,0,0),\qquad \lambda=1.2,\qquad A=0.8,
\]

the special isotropic shell occurs at

\[
r=\frac{\sqrt2}{\lambda}.
\]

Numerical equal-solid-angle quadrature gives a coefficient of variation of approximately

\[
4.6\times10^{-16}
\]

when the shell is centered at `c`, but approximately `3.29628` when the same-radius shell is centered at the old origin.

This is a direct witness that fixed-origin shell diagnostics can misrepresent translated structures.

## 2. Translation-complete DSD target

The centered descriptor should therefore be replaced, at proof level, by an all-center family or envelope such as

\[
\mathcal D_{\rm all}(t)
=
\sup_{x_0\in\mathbb R^3}\sup_{r>0}
\mathcal D(x_0,r,t).
\]

This is still a **CONJECTURE / TARGET**. No global estimate for it has been proved.

## 3. Static superposition versus dynamic independence

Formation-compatible or linear velocity composition does not imply that Navier–Stokes evolution decomposes into independent channel trajectories.

Let `u_1` and `u_2` be two smooth divergence-free benchmark seeds. Then

\[
\nabla\cdot(u_1+u_2)=0.
\]

However the pressure/advection closure source

\[
Q(u)=\sum_{i,j}\partial_i u_j\,\partial_j u_i
\]

is quadratic. Therefore

\[
Q(u_1+u_2)
=Q(u_1)+Q(u_2)+Q_{\rm cross}(u_1,u_2).
\]

For the displayed benchmark pair, symbolic algebra gives a nonzero `Q_cross`; at the test point `(2/5,1/5,-3/10)` it evaluates to approximately

\[
-14.1790480169.
\]

Thus the off-diagonal interaction cannot be discarded.

## 4. DSD matrix/channel interpretation

In a multi-component DSD representation, the diagonal blocks may store each component seed's self terms while the off-diagonal blocks must retain the nonlinear cross couplings generated by the Navier–Stokes advection/pressure closure.

Schematic form:

\[
\mathcal R
=
\begin{pmatrix}
R_{11} & R_{12} & \cdots\\
R_{21} & R_{22} & \cdots\\
\vdots & \vdots & \ddots
\end{pmatrix},
\]

where `R_ii` represents self evolution and `R_ij` represents interaction between distinct component/channel structures.

The matrix dimension here is an interaction bookkeeping dimension and must not be confused with spatial rank.

## 5. Immediate implication for the proof program

The next proof-oriented descriptor must satisfy both conditions:

1. **translation completeness** — no preferred origin may be essential;
2. **interaction completeness** — nonlinear cross terms must not vanish merely because a static aggregate or linear decomposition hides them.

A candidate failing either condition is to be marked **FAILED ROUTE**.

## 6. Computational support

The executable

`src/translation_coupling_baseline.py`

and its regression test verify:

- translated-center recovery of the special shell;
- failure of the same shell diagnostic about an unrelated fixed origin;
- divergence-free preservation under benchmark superposition;
- existence of a nonzero nonlinear `Q_cross` term.

These finite benchmarks do not establish the corresponding claims for arbitrary admissible initial data.
