# Material turnover as threshold crossing

Date: 2026-08-13

Status: **DERIVED MATERIAL-LABEL / LEVEL-SET COAREA LEMMA + OPEN REPEATED-WINDOW SOURCE BUDGET**.

The fixed-positive-volume material-core exclusion leaves one natural escape: the dangerous core can continually replace its material.

This note makes that turnover quantitative.  In the sign-resolved projective branch, material replacement across an oriented-vorticity threshold is not free; it is generated by the material derivative of the axial vorticity component.

---

## 1. Freeze the local axis on one restarted window

Fix a unit vector `n` over one restarted local time window

\[
I=[t_1,t_2].
\]

Define the oriented axial vorticity scalar

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(x,t)=n\cdot\omega(x,t).
}
\]

The use of a constant axis avoids mixing turnover with axis rotation.  A variable-axis commutator is already typed separately in the repository.

Choose fixed threshold values

\[
h_1<h_2.
\]

For each `h in [h_1,h_2]`, define

\[
A_h(t)=\{x:\alpha(x,t)\ge h\}.
\]

On a natural window one may later take `h_i=b_i W(t_1)` with fixed `0<b_1<b_2<1`; freezing the threshold at the restart time prevents an artificial `W'(t)` term.

---

## 2. Material-label representation

Let

\[
X(a,t;t_1)
\]

be the incompressible flow map restarted at `t_1`, so

\[
X(a,t_1;t_1)=a,
\qquad
\det D_aX=1.
\]

Define

\[
\beta(a,t)=\alpha(X(a,t;t_1),t).
\]

Then

\[
\partial_t\beta(a,t)
=D_t\alpha(X(a,t),t).
\]

Because the flow preserves volume, the material image of the initial superlevel set is

\[
X(A_h(t_1),t_2;t_1)
=\{X(a,t_2):\beta(a,t_1)\ge h\}.
\]

The new superlevel set is

\[
A_h(t_2)
=\{X(a,t_2):\beta(a,t_2)\ge h\}.
\]

Thus their symmetric difference measures material labels whose scalar value crossed the threshold an odd number of times.

---

## 3. Threshold-crossing bound

For almost every regular threshold `h`, the total variation of the indicator along each material label bounds the endpoint indicator difference:

\[
\left|
\mathbf 1_{\{\beta(a,t_2)\ge h\}}
-
\mathbf 1_{\{\beta(a,t_1)\ge h\}}
\right|
\le
\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
\delta(\beta(a,t)-h)
|\partial_t\beta(a,t)|dt.
\]

Integrating in `a`, using incompressible change of variables and the level-set coarea identity gives

\[
\boxed{
|A_h(t_2)\triangle X(A_h(t_1),t_2;t_1)|
\le
\int_{t_1}^{t_2}
\int_{\{\alpha=h\}}
\frac{|D_t\alpha|}{|\nabla\alpha|}
\,dA\,dt.
}
\]

This formula counts all crossings on the right, while the endpoint symmetric difference counts only the net material replacement.  Hence the inequality is in the correct direction even when a label crosses the same level several times.

---

## 4. Band-averaged coarea identity

Integrating the crossing bound over `h in [h_1,h_2]` and applying coarea again,

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{h_1}^{h_2}
|A_h(t_2)\triangle X(A_h(t_1),t_2;t_1)|dh\\
&\le
\int_I
\int_{\{h_1<\alpha<h_2\}}
|D_t\alpha|dxdt.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac1{h_2-h_1}
\int_{h_1}^{h_2}
|A_h(t_2)\triangle X(A_h(t_1))|dh
\le
\frac1{h_2-h_1}
\int_I\int_{h_1<\alpha<h_2}|D_t\alpha|.
}
\]

In particular, if the source integral is small, at least one threshold in the band has small material turnover.

Conversely, if every threshold in the band loses/replaces at least a volume `V_turn`, then

\[
\boxed{
\int_I\int_{h_1<\alpha<h_2}|D_t\alpha|dxdt
\ge
(h_2-h_1)V_{\rm turn}.
}
\]

---

## 5. Navier--Stokes source decomposition

For constant `n`, dot the vorticity equation with `n`:

\[
D_t\alpha
=n\cdot(\omega\cdot\nabla)u
+\nu\Delta\alpha.
\]

Since

\[
(\omega\cdot\nabla)u
=S\omega+\Omega\omega
\]

and the antisymmetric part satisfies

\[
\Omega\omega=\frac12\omega\times\omega=0,
\]

we obtain the exact scalar equation

\[
\boxed{
D_t\alpha
=n\cdot S\omega+\nu\Delta\alpha.
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
|D_t\alpha|
\le
|S\omega|+\nu|\Delta\alpha|
\le
|S\omega|+\nu|\Delta\omega|.
}
\]

The material-turnover band therefore obeys

\[
\boxed{
\int_{h_1}^{h_2}
|A_h(t_2)\triangle X(A_h(t_1))|dh
\le
\int_I\int_{h_1<\alpha<h_2}
\bigl(|S\omega|+\nu|\Delta\omega|\bigr)dxdt.
}
\]

Thus turnover is charged to two already typed channels:

1. strain/vortex-stretching production;
2. second-derivative viscous transport.

## 6. Natural-scale dimensionless turnover

Restart at a dangerous value

\[
W_0=W(t_1).
\]

Take

\[
r=aW_0^{-1/2},
\qquad
\tau=\lambda W_0^{-1},
\]

and thresholds

\[
h_i=b_iW_0,
\qquad
0<b_1<b_2<1.
\]

Let `B` be a material/Eulerian comparable region of volume `O(r^3)` over the restarted window.

Define the band-averaged dimensionless turnover fraction

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{\rm mat}
=
\frac1{(h_2-h_1)r^3}
\int_{h_1}^{h_2}
|A_h(t_2)\triangle X(A_h(t_1))|dh.
}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{\rm mat}
\le
\frac1{(h_2-h_1)r^3}
\int_I\int_{B\cap\{h_1<\alpha<h_2\}}
\bigl(|S\omega|+\nu|\Delta\omega|\bigr)dxdt.
}
\]

This quantity is invariant under Navier--Stokes scaling:

- numerator has units `vorticity x volume`;
- denominator `(h_2-h_1)r^3` has the same units.

Thus material turnover enters at the correct critical scale rather than as a dimensional artifact.

---

## 7. `L^2` source form

Let

\[
Q_I=B\times I,
\qquad
|Q_I|\asymp r^3\tau.
\]

Cauchy--Schwarz yields

\[
\int_{Q_I}|S\omega|
\le
|Q_I|^{1/2}
\left(\int_{Q_I}|S\omega|^2\right)^{1/2},
\]

\[
\int_{Q_I}|\Delta\omega|
\le
|Q_I|^{1/2}
\left(\int_{Q_I}|\Delta\omega|^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{\rm mat}
\lesssim
\frac{(r^3\tau)^{1/2}}
{W_0r^3}
\left[
\left(\int_{Q_I}|S\omega|^2\right)^{1/2}
+\nu
\left(\int_{Q_I}|\Delta\omega|^2\right)^{1/2}
\right].
}
\]

With `r~W_0^{-1/2}` and `tau~W_0^{-1}`, the prefactor is

\[
\frac{(r^3\tau)^{1/2}}{W_0r^3}
\asymp
W_0^{-3/4}.
\]

The associated source norms scale as `W_0^{3/4}` at the critical natural scale, so the whole inequality is scale invariant.

The second term belongs naturally to the `k=2` derivative channel; the first belongs to the existing strain/projective S-chain.

## 8. Material-retention consequence

Suppose the dangerous core at each restarted window is defined by some threshold in a fixed relative band `[b_1W_0,b_2W_0]`.

Then there are only two possibilities:

### Low source budget

If the right-hand side above is small, there exists at least one threshold `h` in the band for which the dangerous set retains most of its material.

Repeated low-source windows therefore tend toward the fixed-material-retention branch, which is incompatible with the fixed positive-volume core scenario derived in the previous note.

### High turnover

If every usable threshold has order-one material turnover, then the window must carry order-one critical source cost in

\[
|S\omega|
\quad\text{and/or}\quad
\nu|\Delta\omega|.
\]

Hence continual material replacement is not an independent escape route: it moves the burden into the nonlinear strain chain or the higher-derivative diffusion chain.

---

## 9. Important claim boundary

This lemma does **not** yet prove that a sequence of low-turnover windows contains one fixed positive-volume material subset surviving all the way to `T*`.

A core may lose a small fraction of its material at every window while the product of retention fractions still tends to zero.

Therefore the next problem is quantitative accumulation:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Does finite total source budget imply a positive infinite-window material-retention product?}
}
\]

If turnover fractions `theta_j` satisfy

\[
\sum_j\theta_j<\infty,
\]

then

\[
\prod_j(1-\theta_j)>0,
\]

and a positive material fraction survives.  The missing step is to derive such summability of the turnover fractions from an already finite Navier--Stokes budget.
