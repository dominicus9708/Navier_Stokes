# Conditional local criticality of one nonlinear creation block

Date: 2026-08-14

Status: **CONDITIONAL LOCAL LEMMA / IDENTIFIES A NEW INTERMITTENCY ESCAPE BRANCH / NOT A GLOBAL CLOSURE**.

The exact single-block localization result shows that a surviving adaptive pulse contains at least one matched parabolic block with nonlinear Duhamel variance of order `m`. This note asks what such a block forces if its residual field is quartically regular rather than strongly intermittent.

## 1. Block setup

Consider one matched block of spatial radius `R` and time length comparable to `R^2`, in the co-deforming affine frame.

Let

\[
r=U-a'-Ly
\]

be the residual velocity and

\[
g=\nabla r=\nabla U-L.
\]

Assume throughout the block

\[
\mathcal B_R[g]\le m.
\]

Assume the deterministic affine part has been absorbed into the homogeneous affine propagator, so the genuinely new residual Duhamel term is generated by the residual quadratic interaction, with the already separated far-pressure error negligible on the mesoscopic branch.

## 2. Quartic regularity hypothesis

Assume the local Gaussian or equivalent bounded-enlargement norms satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\|r\|_{L^4_R}
\le C R\sqrt m,
\qquad
\|g\|_{L^4_R}
\le C\sqrt m.
}
\]

These are the scale-natural quartic bounds corresponding to residual velocity amplitude `R sqrt(m)` and residual-gradient amplitude `sqrt(m)`.

They hold automatically for a fixed low Hermite-degree core after Gaussian smoothing. They are **not** asserted here for the full unsmoothed residual field solely from low second-order curvature; a sufficiently small but very high Hermite tail may violate them. Such failure is therefore kept as a separate intermittency branch rather than hidden inside the estimate.

Under the quartic bounds,

\[
\|r\otimes g\|_{L^2_R}
\le
\|r\|_{L^4_R}\|g\|_{L^4_R}
\lesssim
R m.
\]

## 3. Duhamel estimate

Write the residual nonlinear contribution schematically in one-derivative form

\[
Q_g(t_c)
=
-\int_{t_p}^{t_c}
\nabla e^{\nu(t_c-s)\Delta}
\mathbb P\,[r\cdot g](s)\,ds,
\]

where affine-linear pieces belong to the homogeneous propagator.

For the near-field part, standard heat smoothing gives

\[
\|\nabla e^{\nu\delta\Delta}F\|_2
\lesssim
\delta^{-1/2}\|F\|_2.
\]

The singularity is integrable:

\[
\int_0^{CR^2}\delta^{-1/2}d\delta
\lesssim R.
\]

Hence the scale-normalized residual-gradient creation satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sqrt{B_Q}
\lesssim
R^2m
}
\]

up to bounded-affine, near-pressure localization, and fixed enlargement constants.

## 4. Critical consequence

The exact single-block localization gives at one block

\[
B_Q\gtrsim m.
\]

Combining with the conditional upper bound,

\[
\sqrt m
\lesssim
R^2m.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R^2\sqrt m\gtrsim1
}
\]

or equivalently

\[
\boxed{
mR^4\gtrsim1.}
\]

The quantity

\[
\mathcal C_R:=R^2\sqrt m
\]

is exactly the same scale-invariant local-velocity quantity that appeared in the ancient-`L^3` audit through

\[
\|r\|_{L^3(B_{CR})}
\lesssim
R^2\sqrt B.
\]

Thus a quartically regular low-chaos block cannot generate an order-`m` residual pulse while remaining strictly subcritical in the local `L^3` proxy.

## 5. Creation-scale lower bound

With

\[
m=W^{-1/3}\Lambda,
\]

one obtains

\[
\boxed{
R\gtrsim
m^{-1/4}
=
W^{1/12}\Lambda^{-1/4}.
}
\]

This does not close the existing mesoscopic corridor, because that corridor already begins above approximately `W^(1/10)`, where `R^2 sqrt(m)` is strongly supercritical.

The result is nevertheless diagnostic: **fresh nonlinear generation and critical-velocity activity are the same local scale mechanism on the quartically regular branch.**

## 6. New local trichotomy

For the single order-`m` nonlinear creation block, at least one of the following must occur:

1. **critical/supercritical quartically regular creation**
   \[
   mR^4\gtrsim1;
   \]
2. **quartic intermittency / high-Hermite tail dominance**, so the natural `L4` bounds fail despite small `L2` variance;
3. **affine or pressure localization failure**, including a non-negligible far harmonic pressure contribution or loss of the bounded-affine propagator assumptions.

The second branch is genuinely new and must not be silently identified with ordinary low curvature. A small high-degree Hermite tail can carry little `L2` variance while having a much larger quartic footprint.

## 7. Next rigorous target

To turn this conditional lemma into a stronger theorem, one needs one of:

- a blockwise quartic estimate derived from the existing Hermite defect plus first-hitting BMO control;
- a proof that quartic intermittency necessarily contributes a quantitatively non-summable high-curvature or scale-packing budget;
- a vorticity-form version in which the pressure disappears and the corresponding high-frequency tail can be charged directly to the viscous `Delta omega` lane.
