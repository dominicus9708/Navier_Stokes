## 1. Nested critical-mass balances and the double-counting warning

Let `V` be the mean/moving-frame relative velocity and let

\[
e_3=\frac{|V|^3}{3}.
\]

For a radial cutoff `chi_R` supported in `B_{2R}` and equal to one on `B_R`, the local `L^3` balance has the schematic exact form

\[
\frac{d}{ds}M_3(R,s)+\nu D_3(R,s)
=\mathcal P_3(R,s)+\mathcal F_3(R,s),
\]

with

\[
M_3(R,s)=\int\chi_R e_3,
\]

where `P_3` is interior pressure work and `F_3` is the shell flux generated by `grad chi_R`.

Suppose a terminal dangerous core gives

\[
M_3(R,T)\ge m_*>0
\]

for all `1 <= R <= R_*`, while a sufficiently early smooth physical slice becomes negligible in every shrinking physical ball corresponding to these normalized radii.

Summing the nested balances over `R=2^k` appears to produce an `m_* log R_*` lower bound.  However, the same interior pressure/residual production inside `B_1` appears in every larger `P_3(2^k)`.  Therefore this raw sum repeatedly counts the same core event.

**Conclusion:** a logarithmic gain is not valid for the entire source-plus-flux balance without first separating core generation from scale-to-scale transport.

## 2. Genuine shell-transport logarithmic branch

Assume that core/interior generation supplies less than a fixed fraction of the terminal critical mass increment.  The remaining mass must be transported inward across each parent scale.

Choose disjoint dyadic annuli

\[
A_k=B_{2^{k+1}R_0}\setminus B_{2^kR_0},
\qquad k=0,\ldots,K-1.
\]

If a fixed amount `c_*>0` of critical mass has to cross every scale, then the integrated shell fluxes satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}
\int_I |\mathcal F_{3,k}(s)|\,ds
\gtrsim c_*K.
}
\]

Since

\[
K\simeq \log_2\frac{R_*}{R_0},
\]

this is the genuine transport-side logarithmic gain

\[
\boxed{
\sum_k\int_I|\mathcal F_{3,k}|\,ds
\gtrsim c_*\log\frac{R_*}{R_0}.
}
\]

The annuli are disjoint, so this is not the nested-source double counting described above.

For a standard radial cutoff, the advective part obeys

\[
|\mathcal F_{\rm adv,k}|
\lesssim
R_k^{-1}\int_{A_k}|V|^4,
\]

and hence the ladder sum is comparable to a critical weighted bulk quantity of the form

\[
\sum_k R_k^{-1}\int_{A_k}|V|^4
\sim
\int_{R_0<|y|<R_*}\frac{|V(y)|^4}{|y|}\,dy.
\]

Analogous weighted annular forms occur for the pressure and viscous pieces.

This identifies the surviving shell branch as a scale-critical weighted-flux problem.  No finite global budget for this weighted flux has been established here.

## 3. Exact two-scale affine ANOVA

Let

\[
g(y)=\nabla U(y)
\]

be viewed as a matrix-valued field with Frobenius inner product.  For concentric balls define

\[
L_R=\fint_{B_R}g,
\qquad
L_{2R}=\fint_{B_{2R}}g,
\]

and let

\[
A_R=B_{2R}\setminus B_R,
\qquad
L_A=\fint_{A_R}g.
\]

The exact Hilbert-space ANOVA identity is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\int_{B_{2R}}|g-L_{2R}|^2
={}&
\int_{B_R}|g-L_R|^2
+\int_{A_R}|g-L_A|^2\\
&+\frac{|B_R||A_R|}{|B_{2R}|}
|L_R-L_A|^2.
\end{aligned}
\]

In three dimensions

\[
|B_{2R}|=8|B_R|,
\qquad
|A_R|=7|B_R|.
\]

Since

\[
L_R-L_{2R}
=\frac{|A_R|}{|B_{2R}|}(L_R-L_A),
\]

we obtain

\[
\boxed{
\int_{B_{2R}}|g-L_{2R}|^2
\ge
\frac87|B_R|\,|L_R-L_{2R}|^2.
}
\]

Thus a large change of the optimal affine representative between two adjacent parent scales is paid by non-affinity variance.

## 4. Dyadic martingale square-sum

Fix a largest ball `B_{R_K}` with `R_k=2^kR_0`.  Refine the radial partition successively by splitting the current inner ball into its smaller inner ball plus the next annulus.

The corresponding conditional expectations of `g` form an `L^2` martingale.  Its increments are pairwise orthogonal.  Consequently the affine-mean changes along the distinguished inner chain obey

\[
\boxed{

\int_{B_{R_K}}|g-L_{R_K}|^2,

This is the scale-ladder Pythagorean ledger:

\[
\boxed{

It is an exact static-aggregation identity and does not alter the Navier--Stokes equation.
