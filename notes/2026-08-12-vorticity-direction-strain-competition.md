# Vorticity magnitude: direction-gradient versus strain-stretching competition

Date: 2026-08-12

Status: **DERIVED IDENTITY + CONDITIONAL BLOW-UP CERTIFICATE + OPEN CROSS-GATE ESTIMATE**.

This note couples two residual-class channels that had previously been kept separate: vorticity-direction irregularity and strain alignment.

The identity itself is standard vector calculus; no novelty is claimed.

## 1. Magnitude-direction decomposition

On the set where

\[
\omega\neq0,
\]

write

\[
\rho=|\omega|,
\qquad
\xi=\frac{\omega}{|\omega|}.
\]

The vorticity equation is

\[
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)\omega
=S\omega+\nu\Delta\omega,
\]

where

\[
S=\frac12(\nabla u+\nabla u^T).
\]

Dotting with `xi` gives

\[
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)\rho
=
\rho\,\xi^TS\xi
+\nu\,\xi\cdot\Delta(\rho\xi).
\]

Since `|xi|=1`,

\[
\xi\cdot\partial_j\xi=0,
\qquad
\xi\cdot\Delta\xi=-|\nabla\xi|^2,
\]

and therefore

\[
\xi\cdot\Delta(\rho\xi)
=
\Delta\rho-ho|\nabla\xi|^2.
\]

Define

\[
\gamma=\xi^TS\xi.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla-\nu\Delta)\rho
=
\rho\left(
\gamma-
u|\nabla\xi|^2
\right).
}
\]

The two geometric effects enter with opposite signs:

- `gamma`: vortex stretching by strain;
- `nu |grad xi|^2`: diffusion penalty generated by variation of vorticity direction.

## 2. Maximum-vorticity growth channel

Let

\[
W(t)=\|\omega(t)\|_\infty
\]

on the smooth lifespan, and let

\[
\mathcal M(t)=\{x:|\omega(x,t)|=W(t)\}.
\]

At a nonzero spatial maximum of `rho`,

\[
\nabla\rho=0,
\qquad
\Delta\rho\le0.
\]

Thus the upper Dini derivative satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D^+\log W(t)
\le
\mathcal G(t)
}
\]

with

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal G(t)
=
\sup_{x\in\mathcal M(t)}
\left(
\gamma(x,t)-\nu|\nabla\xi(x,t)|^2
\right)_+.
}
\]

If `W(t)` becomes infinite at a finite endpoint `T*`, then necessarily

\[
\boxed{
\int^{T^*}\mathcal G(t)\,dt=\infty.
}
\]

This is a necessary condition obtained from the magnitude equation, not a sufficient blow-up criterion.

The time integral is Navier--Stokes scale invariant because both `gamma` and `|grad xi|^2` scale like inverse time.

## 3. Residual-class consequence

A hypothetical singularity that evades a vorticity-direction coherence theorem by developing strong directional variation cannot treat that variation as dynamically free.

Large

\[
|\nabla\xi|^2
\]

creates a negative term in the vorticity-magnitude equation.  To retain positive growth at a maximum, the strain alignment must satisfy

\[
\boxed{
\gamma
>
\nu|\nabla\xi|^2
}
\]

there, and finite-time blow-up requires the positive excess to have nonintegrable accumulated strength along maximum-vorticity events.

Thus the residual requirements

\[
G_{\rm dir}^{c}
\quad\text{and}\quad
G_{\rm strain}^{c}
\]

are coupled through an exact PDE identity.

## 4. Strain eigenframe decomposition

Let

\[
\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\lambda_3
\]

be the eigenvalues of `S` with orthonormal eigenvectors `e_i`, and define

\[
a_i^2=(\xi\cdot e_i)^2,
\qquad
\sum_i a_i^2=1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\gamma
=
\sum_{i=1}^3\lambda_i a_i^2.
}
\]

Since the first two eigenvalues are no larger than `lambda_2^+`,

\[
\boxed{
\gamma
\le
\lambda_2^+(1-a_3^2)
+
\lambda_3a_3^2.
}
\]

Hence a large positive `gamma` requires at least one of two structures:

1. a significant positive middle-eigenvalue contribution;
2. significant alignment with the most extensional eigenvector `e_3`, weighted by `lambda_3`.

When `lambda_2<=0`, positivity of `gamma` already forces quantitative extensional alignment:

\[
\gamma
\le
\lambda_2(1-a_3^2)+\lambda_3a_3^2,
\]

so

\[
\gamma>0
\Longrightarrow
\boxed{
 a_3^2>
\frac{-\lambda_2}{\lambda_3-\lambda_2}
}
\]

whenever `lambda_3>lambda_2`.

Thus the middle-eigenvalue and alignment channels are not redundant, but they form an exact either/or decomposition of positive stretching.

Important typing rules:

- `xi`, `grad xi`, `gamma`, and `a_i` are inapplicable where `omega=0`;
- at maximum-vorticity points in a blow-up scenario, `omega!=0`, so the competition block is applicable;
- `lambda_2^+` and `lambda_3 a_3^2` must not be collapsed into one scalar before an estimate is proved.

## 6. Cross-gate target

The residual class can now be sharpened.

To sustain a singularity, the flow must simultaneously

1. keep intense vorticity non-sparse at the natural scale;
2. avoid sufficient direction coherence;
3. nevertheless prevent the direction-gradient diffusion penalty from dominating;
4. produce nonintegrable positive excess `G` at maximum-vorticity events;
5. do so through either positive middle strain or strong most-extensional alignment (or both).

A useful next theorem would control

\[
\int\mathcal G(t)dt
\]

using one of the already finite/critical DSD cascade channels.  No such estimate is established here.
