## 1. Pressure remains nonlocal, but differences improve the far field

For incompressible Navier--Stokes on `R^3`,

\[
-\Delta p=\partial_i\partial_j(u_i u_j).
\]

The pressure kernel `K_ij` is homogeneous of degree `-3`, while its gradient is homogeneous of degree `-4`.

For two nearby points `x,y` and a far source point `z`, the mean-value theorem gives schematically

\[
|K(x-z)-K(y-z)|
\le C|x-y|\,|z-X|^{-4},
\]

and

\[
|\nabla K(x-z)-\nabla K(y-z)|
\le C|x-y|\,|z-X|^{-5},
\]

provided `x,y` remain in a local neighborhood of `X` and `z` is separated from that neighborhood.

Thus taking a pressure or pressure-gradient **difference** gains one power of far-field decay.

This is consistent with standard local-energy pressure decompositions, where the far pressure is written using a kernel difference relative to a local center.

## 2. Near/far split for the material pressure-difference channel

For a restartable material cell of scale `ell`, split

\[
\delta(\nabla p)
=
\delta(\nabla p)_{\rm near}
+
\delta(\nabla p)_{\rm far}.
\]

The cutoff radius should be chosen as a fixed multiple of the actual current-cell diameter.  If recent material distortion is controlled, this diameter is comparable to `ell`.

The far part has the schematic estimate

\[
|\delta(\nabla p)_{\rm far}|
\le
C d
\int_{\rm far}
\frac{|u(z,t)|^2}{|z-X|^5}\,dz,
\]

where `d=|x-y|`.

## 3. Dyadic critical-energy estimate

Assume that on dyadic radii around the local region,

\[
\int_{B_R(X)}|u|^2dx
\le M R,
\]

where `M` is the critical `L^2`-Morrey energy size.

On a shell of radius `R_k=2^kR`,

\[
d\int_{A_k}
\frac{|u|^2}{|z-X|^5}dz
\lesssim

\]

Summing gives

\[
|\delta(\nabla p)_{\rm far}|
\lesssim
C d M R^{-4}.
\]

When `d` and `R` are both comparable to `ell`, this has the critical size

\[
|\delta(\nabla p)_{\rm far}|
\lesssim C M\ell^{-3}.

The far-field model estimate then yields schematically

\[
\boxed{

Recall

\[

Thus the principal pressure proof obligation moves toward the near field.
