## 1. Affine decomposition

Write the velocity near the Gaussian center as

\[
U(x,t)
=a'(t)+L(t)(x-a(t))+r(x,t).
\]

Let the affine flow matrix satisfy

\[
F'(t)=L(t)F(t),
\qquad
F(t_0)=I.
\]

Since `div U=0`,

\[
\operatorname{tr}L=0,
\]

so

\[
\det F=1.
\]

Introduce co-affine coordinates

\[
x=a(t)+F(t)z.
\]

## 2. Cauchy vorticity variable

Define

\[
\widetilde\Omega(z,t)
=F(t)^{-1}\Omega(a(t)+F(t)z,t).
\]

For the pure affine vorticity equation

\[
\partial_t\Omega
+(L(x-a)\cdot\nabla)\Omega
=L\Omega+\nu\Delta\Omega,
\]

the coordinate derivative and the derivative of `F^{-1}` cancel the affine transport and affine stretching exactly.

The transformed equation is

\[
\boxed{
\partial_t\widetilde\Omega
=
\nu\nabla_z\cdot
\bigl(G(t)\nabla_z\widetilde\Omega\bigr),
}
\]

where

\[
G(t)=F(t)^{-1}F(t)^{-T}.
\]

Thus pure affine inheritance is anisotropic heat in the Cauchy frame.

Over a block `[t_p,t_c]`, the pure affine heat covariance in `z` is

\[
\boxed{

## 4. The distinct mean-vorticity linear coupling

When the residual velocity is restored, the perturbation-vorticity equation also contains the term

\[
\bar\Omega\cdot\nabla r,
\]

where `barOmega` is the spatially uniform affine/mean vorticity and `r` is recovered from residual vorticity by Biot--Savart.

This term is linear in the residual state and is **not** the same as affine transport/stretch.

At frozen Euclidean coefficients, let

\[
\eta=\nabla\times r.
\]

Then in Fourier variables

\[
\widehat r(k)
=
\frac{i k\times\widehat\eta(k)}{|k|^2}.
\]

Hence

\[
\widehat{(\bar\Omega\cdot\nabla r)}
=
-\frac{\bar\Omega\cdot k}{|k|^2}
\bigl(k\times\widehat\eta(k)\bigr).
\]

For every fixed `k`, the map

\[
v\mapsto k\times v
\]

is skew-symmetric. The scalar multiplier is real. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Re\left[
\widehat\eta(k)^*\cdot
\widehat{(\bar\Omega\cdot\nabla r)}(k)
\right]=0.
}
\]

So the frozen mean-vorticity coupling preserves `|eta_hat(k)|^2` mode by mode.

It is a rotation/redistribution operator, not an `L2` amplitude-production operator.
