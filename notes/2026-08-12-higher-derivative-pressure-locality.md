# Higher-derivative remote-pressure locality across physical scales

This note couples the physical-scale index `j` to the derivative-order index `k` introduced in the higher-derivative chain integration note.

It is a pressure-kernel estimate only.  It does not reproduce or strengthen the full Grujic--Xu derivative-chain theorem by itself.

## 1. Pressure kernel and derivatives

In whole space, the canonical pressure can be represented schematically as

\[
p(x)=\operatorname{p.v.}\int K_{ij}(x-z)u_i(z)u_j(z)\,dz,
\]

where `K_ij` is homogeneous of degree `-3`.

For a spatial derivative block `D^(k)`, the remote part may be differentiated on the kernel away from the singularity:

\[
D^{(k)}p_{\rm rem}(x)
=
\int_{|z-X|\ge8\ell}
D^{(k)}K_{ij}(x-z)u_i(z)u_j(z)\,dz.
\]

Since

\[
|D^{(k+m)}K(y)|\lesssim |y|^{-3-k-m},
\]

all estimates below are ordinary smooth-kernel estimates on the separated remote region.

## 2. Remove the dynamically irrelevant affine part

For a differentiated divergence-free velocity block, the corresponding localized pressure work again has the form

\[
\int D^{(k)}p\,w\cdot\nabla\phi_\ell,
\qquad
\nabla\cdot w=0,
\]

with the coherent weighted mean of `w` subtracted when forming the internal-oscillation channel.

Consequently constant and linear spatial pieces of `D^(k)p` make no contribution, by the same divergence-free / weighted-mean-zero cancellation used at order zero.

Let

\[
L_X[D^{(k)}p_{\rm rem}]
\]

be the affine Taylor polynomial at `X`.  Taylor's theorem gives, for `x in B_{2 ell}(X)`,

\[
\boxed{
|D^{(k)}p_{\rm rem}(x)-L_X[D^{(k)}p_{\rm rem}](x)|
\lesssim
\ell^2
\int_{|z-X|\ge8\ell}
\frac{|u(z)|^2}{|z-X|^{5+k}}dz.
}
\]

## 3. Scale-invariant derivative-pressure tail

Under the Navier--Stokes scaling,

\[
D^{(k)}p
\mapsto
\lambda^{k+2}D^{(k)}p,
\]

so

\[
\|D^{(k)}p\|_{L^{3/2}(B_\ell)}
\]

scales like `lambda^k`.  Therefore define

\[
\boxed{
H_{\ell,k}
=
\ell^k
\inf_{\text{affine }L}
\|D^{(k)}p_{\rm rem}-L\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{2\ell}(X))}.
}
\]

This quantity is scale invariant.

The pointwise Taylor estimate and the volume of the inner ball imply

\[
\boxed{
H_{\ell,k}
\lesssim
\ell^{k+4}
\int_{|z-X|\ge8\ell}
\frac{|u(z)|^2}{|z-X|^{5+k}}dz.
}
\]

## 4. Dyadic physical-scale suppression

Let

\[
M_R(X,t)
=
R^{-1}\int_{B_R(X)}|u|^2dx
\]

and use dyadic annuli

\[
A_j=
\{2^j\ell\le|z-X|<2^{j+1}\ell\},
\qquad j\ge3.
\]

On `A_j`,

\[
\int_{A_j}|u|^2
\le
(2^{j+1}\ell)
M_{2^{j+1}\ell}.
\]

Hence

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{\ell,k}
&\lesssim
\ell^{k+4}
\sum_{j\ge3}
(2^j\ell)^{-5-k}
\int_{A_j}|u|^2\\
&\lesssim
\boxed{
\sum_{j\ge3}
2^{-(k+4)j}
M_{2^{j+1}\ell}
}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The fixed factors absorbed into the implicit constant are independent of `j`.

## 5. Cross-index interpretation

The zeroth derivative reproduces the existing remote-pressure decay

\[
k=0:
\qquad
2^{-4j}.
\]

Each additional spatial derivative strengthens the distant-scale suppression by one dyadic power:

\[
\boxed{
(j,k)\text{ remote coupling weight}
\sim
2^{-(k+4)j}.
}
\]

Therefore high derivative levels are progressively less sensitive to a **direct affine-free pressure input from far physical scales**.

This does not imply finite propagation, nor does it remove the pressure generated by nearby scales.

## 6. Macroscopically remote derivative pressure

For fixed physical distance `L>0`, the total finite kinetic energy `E_0` gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{\ell,k}^{|z-X|\ge L}
&\lesssim
\ell^{k+4}
L^{-5-k}E_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus for every fixed derivative order `k`,

\[
\boxed{
H_{\ell,k}^{|z-X|\ge L}
=O(\ell^{k+4})
\to0
\quad(\ell\to0).
}
\]

Higher derivative order accelerates this suppression.

## 7. Relation to the Grujic--Xu external derivative-chain framework

Grujic--Xu study sparseness and analyticity scales of higher spatial derivatives and show that the scaling gap becomes asymptotically critical as the derivative order grows.

The present estimate is **not** their result and does not close their chain.  Its role is narrower:

- `k` indexes the established derivative chain;
- `j` indexes physical scale in the DSD cascade;
- the pressure off-diagonal coupling from distant `j` to a fixed `(j0,k)` block carries the explicit weight `2^{-(k+4)|j-j0|}` in the remote direction.

Thus the two-index DSD matrix has a pressure sector whose far-off-diagonal entries decay more rapidly as derivative order increases.
