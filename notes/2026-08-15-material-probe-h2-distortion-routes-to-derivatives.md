## 1. Probe transport equation

The material adjoint probe satisfies

\[
\boxed{
D_s\psi
:=
(\partial_s+U\cdot\nabla)\psi
=-(\nabla U)^T\psi.
}
\]

Set

\[
A=\nabla U.
\]

For smooth compactly supported probes, the volume-preserving material transport does not itself change Lebesgue measure.

## 2. Zeroth derivative

Taking the `L2` inner product with `psi`,

\[
\frac12\frac d{ds}\|\psi\|_2^2
=-\int \psi\cdot A^T\psi.
\]

The antisymmetric part drops from the quadratic form, so

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{ds}\|\psi\|_2
\le
\|S\|_\infty\|\psi\|_2,
}
\]

where `S=sym A`.

Thus pure coherent rotation does not enlarge the probe norm; zeroth-order material distortion is a strain channel.

## 3. First derivative

Differentiate the transport equation. The material derivative does not commute with `nabla`:

\[
[D_s,\partial_i]
=-(\partial_iU_k)\partial_k.
\]

Therefore schematically

\[
D_s(\nabla\psi)
=
-A^T\nabla\psi
-(\nabla\psi)A
-(\nabla A)^T\psi,
\]

up to the fixed index placements.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{ds}\|\nabla\psi\|_2
\lesssim
\|A\|_\infty\|\nabla\psi\|_2
+
\|\nabla A\|_\infty\|\psi\|_2.
}
\]

Since

\[
\nabla A=\nabla^2U,
\]

first-derivative probe distortion requires strain/rotation gradients, equivalently first derivatives of vorticity up to singular-integral equivalence.

## 4. Second derivative

Differentiate once more. The resulting terms have the schematic types

\[
A\,\nabla^2\psi,
\qquad
(\nabla A)\,\nabla\psi,
\qquad
(\nabla^2A)\,\psi.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{ds}\|\nabla^2\psi\|_2
\lesssim
\|A\|_\infty\|\nabla^2\psi\|_2
+
\|\nabla A\|_\infty\|\nabla\psi\|_2
+
\|\nabla^2A\|_\infty\|\psi\|_2.

\]

Because

\[
\nabla^2A=\nabla^3U,

\[
\boxed{
