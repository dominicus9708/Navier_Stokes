Status: **ELEMENTARY GEOMETRIC ROUTING LEMMA BUILT ON THE STOCHASTIC-KELVIN ANCESTOR LENGTH BARRIER / GLOBAL REGULARITY NOT PROVED**.

## 1. Input from stochastic Kelvin

The preceding stochastic-Kelvin note gives, for every sufficiently late coherent Reynolds-one crossing, an earlier stochastic ancestor loop `C_-` with

\[
\boxed{
L_-
\gtrsim
R\sqrt q.
}
\]

Here

The remaining question is whether `C_-` can evade the spatial-persistence/energy routes merely by being extremely long but packed into a small region.

## 2. Geometric descriptors

Assume first that the stochastic ancestor loop is a simple `C2` embedded closed curve.  This is natural for a stochastic diffeomorphic image of a smooth final loop on the smooth lifespan.

Write

\[
L=L(C_-),
\]

\[
D=\operatorname{diam}(C_-),
\]

and let

\[
\rho_{\rm reach}=\operatorname{reach}(C_-)
\]

be the tubular reach: the largest radius on which the nearest-point projection to the curve is unique.

Small reach can arise from either

1. large curvature;
2. near self-approach of distinct portions of the embedded loop.

---

## 3. Tube-volume estimate

For any

\[
0<r\le c\rho_{\rm reach}
\]

with a fixed sufficiently small universal `c`, the radius-`r` tubular neighborhood of the loop is embedded.

Its volume satisfies

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal T_r(C_-)|
\gtrsim
r^2L.
}
\]

If the loop is contained in a ball of diameter comparable to `D`, then the tube is contained in a slightly enlarged ball, so

\[
|\mathcal T_r(C_-)|
\lesssim
(D+r)^3.
\]

Taking `r=c rho_reach` and treating the trivial case `rho_reach >= D` separately gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\rm reach}^2L
\lesssim
D^3.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac{\rho_{\rm reach}}D
\lesssim
\left(\frac DL\right)^{1/2}.
}
\]

This uses only embedded-tube volume and does not invoke Navier--Stokes.

## 4. Insert the stochastic ancestor length barrier

Since

\[

Thus a stochastic ancestor carrying the required circulation has only two broad geometric possibilities.

### G1. Spatially extended ancestor

Its diameter is comparable to its forced length scale:

\[

Then the ancestor is genuinely spatially non-tight and returns to

\[

\[
\boxed{
\rho_{\rm reach}
\lesssim
\sqrt\eta\,D.
}
\]

Therefore a long ancestor kept inside a much smaller diameter must lose a corresponding fraction of its geometric reach.

## 5. Resolve small reach into two typed channels

For a smooth embedded curve, reach is controlled by both curvature and self-distance.

Schematically,

\[
\rho_{\rm reach}
\lesssim
\min\left\{
\kappa_{\max}^{-1},
\frac12 d_{\rm self}
\right\},
\]

where `d_self` denotes the relevant nonlocal self-approach scale.

Thus G2 splits into

\[
\boxed{
\text{G2a: high curvature}
}
\]

or

\[
\boxed{
\text{G2b: close approach of materially distant curve segments}.
}
\]

### G2a. High curvature

The stochastic flow image of the initially smooth final loop develops a curvature scale much smaller than its macroscopic diameter.

This is a flow-map second-derivative event and returns to the already typed

\[
\boxed{
H^2\text{ material-probe distortion}
\to
\nabla^2u/\nabla^3u\text{ derivative forcing}.
}
\]

### G2b. Near self-approach

Two portions of the ancestor loop that remain distinct under the stochastic diffeomorphism approach to a separation `d_self << D`.

Their relative separation has no direct additive-Brownian contribution because the noise is spatially uniform; relative deformation is produced by the physical velocity gradient sampled along the two trajectories.

Therefore a large ratio

\[
D/d_{\rm self}
\]

requires a corresponding accumulated deformation/strain channel.  At the crude global level,

\[
\boxed{
\int\|S(t)\|_\infty dt
\gtrsim
\log\frac{D}{d_{\rm self}}
}
\]

whenever the relevant separation is generated by a Lipschitz deformation on the smooth interval.

Thus near self-contact is not a free topological escape; it is a pair-compression strain event.
