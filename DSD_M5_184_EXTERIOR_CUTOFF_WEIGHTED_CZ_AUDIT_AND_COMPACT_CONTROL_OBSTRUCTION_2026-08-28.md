## 1. Radial cutoff

Fix `R>0` and choose a smooth radial function `chi_R` satisfying

\[
\chi_R=0\quad (r\le R),
\qquad
\chi_R=1\quad (r\ge2R).
\]

Set

\[
\widetilde Z:=\chi_R Z,
\qquad
\widetilde\eta:=\chi_R\eta.
\]

Both fields are smooth across the artificial inner cut because they vanish identically near the cut.

The entire artificial-boundary issue is thereby replaced by commutators supported in

\[
\boxed{A_R:=\{R<r<2R\}.}
\]

## 2. Exact elliptic cutoff equation

Using

\[
-\Delta Z=\nabla\times\eta,
\]

we obtain

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\Delta(\chi_RZ)
&=\chi_R\nabla\times\eta
-2\nabla\chi_R\cdot\nabla Z
-(\Delta\chi_R)Z\\
&=\nabla\times(\chi_R\eta)+G_R,
\end{aligned}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
G_R
=-\nabla\chi_R\times\eta
-2\nabla\chi_R\cdot\nabla Z
-(\Delta\chi_R)Z
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}G_R\subset A_R.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{-\Delta\widetilde Z=\nabla\times\widetilde\eta+G_R.}
\]

## 3. Exact parabolic cutoff equation

The relative vorticity equation is

\[
\begin{aligned}
P\eta
&+(u^V\cdot\nabla)\eta
-(\eta\cdot\nabla)u^V\\
&+(Z\cdot\nabla)\omega^W
-(\omega^W\cdot\nabla)Z=0,
\end{aligned}
\]

with

\[
P=\partial_t-\nu\Delta.
\]

Multiplying by `chi_R` and commuting derivatives through the cutoff yields

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
P\widetilde\eta
&+(u^V\cdot\nabla)\widetilde\eta
-(\widetilde\eta\cdot\nabla)u^V\\
&+(\widetilde Z\cdot\nabla)\omega^W
-(\omega^W\cdot\nabla)\widetilde Z
=F_R,
\end{aligned}}
\]

where every term of `F_R` contains at least one derivative of `chi_R` and hence

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{supp}F_R\subset A_R.}
\]

The coefficients outside the core are bounded uniformly up to the terminal time.

## 4. What the Lei–Yang–Yuan weighted method supplies

The 2024 backward-uniqueness argument for bounded mild whole-space Navier–Stokes solutions uses a time-singular weight together with a polynomial spatial weight

\[
(1+|x|^2)^{-k}.
\]

A key estimate controls the Calderón–Zygmund/Leray term despite the weight not belonging to the usual `A_p` range.  In schematic form,

\[
\boxed{
\int (1+|x|^2)^{-k}|R\nabla\cdot f|^2
\lesssim
\int (1+|x|^2)^{-k}(|\nabla f|^2+|f|^2)
}
\]

for the relevant range of `k`.

Therefore the pressure/nonlocal projection channel generated by bounded Oseen coefficients is **not** the principal obstruction once the physical pair is globally bounded by cutoff/extension.

This is an important improvement over the naive Gaussian-weight Riesz approach.

## 5. Why the cutoff still does not give backward uniqueness

The weighted estimate applied to the cutoff equation necessarily contains

\[
\|F_R\|_{weighted}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\|G_R\|_{weighted}
\]

or equivalent compact-control terms.

These are not external errors that may simply be sent to zero.  They encode the actual unknown interior/core difference through the annulus.

A generic inhomogeneous parabolic equation

\[
w_t-\Delta w=f
\]

can be driven to `w(T)=0` by a nonzero control `f`.  Requiring `f` to be smooth or flat at the final time does not by itself restore backward uniqueness.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{compact annulus forcing}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{negligible backward forcing}.
}
\]

The spatial polynomial weight may make a translated annulus contribution small, but smallness at large translation gives only quantitative decay, not exact vanishing at any fixed exterior point.

## 7. Exact remaining endogenous-control lemma

The cutoff route would close if one could prove:

### EC — endogenous control exclusion

For the coupled cutoff system

\[
\begin{cases}
P\widetilde\eta+\mathcal L_1(\widetilde\eta,\widetilde Z)=F_R,\\
-\Delta\widetilde Z=\nabla\times\widetilde\eta+G_R,
\end{cases}
\]

with `F_R,G_R` generated exactly by one radial cutoff from an underlying homogeneous same-tail solution, terminal-zero exterior data cannot coexist with a nonzero interior pair.

This is **not** true for arbitrary compact controls.  Any proof must use the algebraic relation between `F_R,G_R` and the underlying homogeneous pair.
