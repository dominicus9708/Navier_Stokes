## 1. New sign-resolved oriented-flux closure

Inside a projectively nearly one-axis intense core, polarity must be tracked separately because `xi tensor xi` cannot distinguish `xi` from `-xi`.

For a constant local axis `n`, signed axial flux is

\[
\Phi_\rho(s)=\int_{D_\rho(s)}\omega\cdot n\,dA.
\]

Divergence-free vorticity gives

\[
\Phi_\rho(s_2)-\Phi_\rho(s_1)
=-\int_{\Sigma_\rho}\omega_\perp\cdot\nu_\perp dS.
\]

Cylindrical coarea yields a radius `rho_* in [r,2r]` for which

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathcal A_{r,2r;L}}|\omega_\perp|^2dx
\ge
\frac{|\Delta\Phi_{\rho_*}|^2}{4\pi L}.
}
\]

Thus robust natural-scale flux termination over `L=O(r)` costs order-one scale-invariant off-axis enstrophy.

If flux persists instead,

\[
\boxed{
r\int_{\rm tube}|\omega|^2dx
\gtrsim L/r.
}
\]

If radial cancellation prevents robust flux, an opposite-polarity annular population is forced and the mixed-polarity/palinstrophy branch reappears.

## 3. Material vorticity flux can change only viscously

For a material surface `S(t)`,

\[
\Phi_S(t)=\int_{S(t)}\omega\cdot n\,dA

\[
\boxed{
\frac{d\Phi_S}{dt}

Thus material advection/stretching does not destroy or create vorticity flux directly.  Flux change is a viscous boundary derivative effect.

## 8. Necessary correction: turnover versus pruning

Fixed positive material volume being impossible does **not** mean new material must always be recruited.

If

\[
W_{j+1}=qW_j,
\]

then natural core volume shrinks by

\[
\boxed{
V_{j+1}/V_j\asymp q^{-3/2}.
}
\]

The next core can simply use a smaller subset of old dangerous material.

Therefore retain two typed branches:

1. **R — recruitment / turnover:** new labels enter the dangerous threshold;
2. **P — pruning / selection:** the dangerous set chooses a shrinking subset of old labels.

The correct inter-window overlap is normalized by the **new** core:

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal O_{j\to j+1}
=
\frac{|C_{j+1}\cap X(C_j,t_{j+1};t_j)|}{|C_{j+1}|}.
}
\]

Low overlap activates the Cauchy-turnover cost.  High overlap activates the pruning branch.
