## 2. Exact gradient and strain equations

Write

\[
G:=\nabla u,
\qquad
S:=\frac12(G+G^T).
\]

Differentiating the incompressible Navier--Stokes equation gives

\[
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)G
+G^2
+\nabla^2p
=\nu\Delta G.
\]

Taking symmetric parts,

\[
\boxed{
(\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla)S
=\nu\Delta S
-\operatorname{sym}(G^2)
-\nabla^2p.
}
\]

Therefore an exterior contribution can be invisible in `S` at the present time yet affect `S_t` instantly through a nonzero exterior pressure Hessian.

## 3. Exterior pressure is harmonic in a source-free observation ball after source localization

Use the usual near/far pressure decomposition relative to a parent ball.

The far pressure is generated by sources outside the parent ball. Inside the inner observation ball its source is absent, so after subtracting the local pressure gauge one has

\[
\Delta p_{far}=0
\]

there.

Hence

\[
p_{far}(x)
=b_0+b_1\cdot x
+\sum_{m\ge2}\pi_m(x)
\]

with homogeneous harmonic polynomials `pi_m`.

The constant and linear terms have zero Hessian.

The first dynamically deforming pressure term is degree two:

\[
\nabla^2\pi_2=\text{constant symmetric trace-free matrix}.
\]

Higher modes create higher spatial derivatives.
