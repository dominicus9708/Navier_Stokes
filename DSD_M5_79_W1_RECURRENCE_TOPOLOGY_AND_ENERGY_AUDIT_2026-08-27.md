\[

\[

## 3. What the global L2 identity would be if it were legitimate

Assume temporarily that

\[
U(s)\in L^2(\mathbb R^3),
\qquad
\nabla U(s)\in L^2(\mathbb R^3),
\]

with enough decay to justify all integrations by parts.

Multiplying the Leray equation by `U` and integrating gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\frac d{ds}\|U(s)\|_2^2
+\nu\|\nabla U(s)\|_2^2
-\frac14\|U(s)\|_2^2
=0.
}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{ds}\|U\|_2^2
=
\frac12\|U\|_2^2
-2\nu\|\nabla U\|_2^2.
}
\]

Hence the autonomous Leray energy

\[
\|U\|_2^2
\]

is not monotone in general.

Therefore recurrence of `U(s)` is not contradicted by this identity.

## 4. The decreasing quantity is explicitly s-dependent

Multiplying the preceding identity by `e^{-s/2}` gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac d{ds}
\left(e^{-s/2}\|U(s)\|_2^2\right)
=
-2\nu e^{-s/2}\|\nabla U(s)\|_2^2
\le0.
}
\]

But

\[
\|u(t)\|_2^2
=
\tau^{1/2}\|U(s)\|_2^2
=
e^{-s/2}\|U(s)\|_2^2.
\]

Thus this is exactly the ordinary physical kinetic-energy law written in Leray variables.

It is an explicitly time-weighted quantity, not an autonomous state functional on the Leray phase space.

A periodic profile can therefore satisfy

\[
U(s+S)=U(s)
\]

while

\[
e^{-(s+S)/2}\|U(s+S)\|_2^2
=e^{-S/2}e^{-s/2}\|U(s)\|_2^2.
\]

There is no recurrence contradiction.
