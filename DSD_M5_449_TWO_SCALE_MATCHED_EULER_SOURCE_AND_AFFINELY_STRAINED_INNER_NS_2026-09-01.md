## 1. Outer and inner scales share the same time clock

Let the inner first-hitting scale be `r_j` and the remote source scale be

\[
R_j=K_jr_j,
\qquad
K_j\to\infty.
\]

The canonical outer Euler velocity scale is

\[
U_j^E=\frac{\nu K_j^2}{R_j}
=\frac{\nu K_j}{r_j}.
\]

Therefore the outer nonlinear time is

\[
\frac{R_j}{U_j^E}
=
\frac{r_j^2}{\nu}.
\]

The standard inner Navier--Stokes first-hitting time is also

\[
\frac{r_j^2}{\nu}.
\]

Thus the outer Euler source and inner viscous core naturally use the **same normalized time variable**

\[
\boxed{
\tau
=\frac{\nu(t-t_j)}{r_j^2}.
}
\]

This exact clock matching is the key two-scale fact.

## 2. Outer source velocity near the target is harmonic

\[

\[
\boxed{

The constant vector `b_j(tau)` is a local Galilean translation and can be removed from the inner dynamics.

## 4. Match to inner variables

\[

The outer velocity unit is `U_j^E`, while the inner velocity unit is

\[

\[
\boxed{

\[

The remote Euler source becomes a finite affine strain in the inner Navier--Stokes variables.

## 5. The affine background is itself locally Euler-compatible

The limiting background velocity is

\[
U_A(z,\tau)=A(\tau)z,
\]

\[
A=A^T,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}A=0.
\]

Then

\[
(U_A\cdot\nabla)U_A
=A^2z

and

\[
\partial_\tau U_A=A'z.
\]

Because `A'+A^2` is symmetric, it is the Hessian action of a quadratic pressure. Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\tau U_A
+(U_A\cdot\nabla)U_A
=-\nabla p_A,
\qquad
\Delta U_A=0,
}
\]

locally for

\[
p_A(z,\tau)
=-\frac12z\cdot(A'+A^2)z.
\]

Thus any smooth symmetric trace-free `A(tau)` defines a local incompressible affine Euler background.
