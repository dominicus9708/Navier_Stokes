## 1. Exterior-cloud field inside a source-free ball

Fix a satellite-centered ball

\[
B_R=B_R(0)
\]

and suppose the cloud sources being classified lie outside `B_R`.

Let `h` denote only the velocity contribution of those exterior vorticity sources inside `B_R`.

Because no vorticity source of this exterior component is present in `B_R`,

\[
\nabla\times h=0
\]

there. Incompressibility also gives

\[
\nabla\cdot h=0.
\]

Therefore each component of `h` is harmonic, and on a simply connected ball there exists a scalar harmonic potential `phi` such that

\[
\boxed{
h=\nabla\phi,
\qquad
\Delta\phi=0
\quad\text{in }B_R.
}
\]

An additive constant in `h` is a Galilean velocity and does not affect vorticity or strain.

## 2. Interior harmonic expansion

Around the satellite center write the regular solid-harmonic expansion

\[
\phi(r,\theta)
= a_0
+ a_1\cdot x
+\sum_{m\ge2}\phi_m(x),
\]

where `phi_m` is a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree `m`.

Then

\[
h= a_1
+\sum_{m\ge2}\nabla\phi_m.
\]

The `m=1` term gives only a constant velocity.

The first deforming term is `m=2`, for which

\[
\nabla\phi_2=Mx,
\qquad
M=M^T,
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}M=0.
\]

This is precisely an ambient affine strain.

Higher `m` give non-affine harmonic gradients whose first nonzero derivative order is finite.

## 3. Lowest nonzero multipole gives a finite-order witness

Suppose the first nonzero deforming multipole has degree `m_*>=2`.

Then

\[
D^{m_*-1}h(0)\neq0,
\]

while all lower deforming derivatives vanish appropriately.

Therefore dense angular cancellation cannot hide a nontrivial exterior effect from **all** finite derivative levels unless every deforming harmonic coefficient vanishes.

In proof-tree language,

\[
\boxed{
\exists m_*\ge2\text{ first nonzero multipole}
\Longrightarrow
H_{harm,m_*}
}
\]

once the corresponding scale-normalized derivative exceeds the retained corridor ceiling.

The `m_*=2` case is the existing ambient-strain branch.

## 4. Exact silent exterior

If every deforming multipole vanishes,

\[
\phi_m=0
\qquad(m\ge2),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{h\equiv a_1\quad\text{on }B_R.}
\]

After a Galilean normalization,

\[
\boxed{h\equiv0\quad\text{on }B_R.}
\]

Thus an exact silent cloud is not merely a set for which the strain tensors cancel at the center. It is a source configuration whose entire exterior velocity field is locally constant throughout the screened ball.

All vorticity and strain dynamics inside the ball are then generated by the interior component itself, up to pressure/gauge effects associated with uniform frame motion.

## 5. Pressure/gauge scope

A spatially constant exterior velocity may be removed by a Galilean frame.

A purely spatially affine pressure contribution corresponding to a uniform acceleration can alter the local translation of the frame but does not create vorticity or strain.

Hence the screened classification is intended for deformation/vorticity dynamics:

\[
\boxed{
\text{exact silent exterior}
\Rightarrow
\text{no exterior strain or vorticity forcing inside the screened ball}.
}
\]

This statement does not erase boundary transport when the screened region changes in time.

## 6. Relation to M5-299 dense cancellation

M5-299 described dynamic cancellation of many leading trace-free strain tensors.

The harmonic formulation sharpens that picture:

- cancellation of the degree-2 harmonic coefficient removes ambient affine strain;
- cancellation of degree 3 removes the next derivative mode;

Therefore the truly silent branch is the intersection of an infinite hierarchy of moment cancellations.

But this is not forbidden algebraically. Exterior source distributions with no interior field are an inverse-source nullspace phenomenon.

Hence codimension counting is not a proof.
