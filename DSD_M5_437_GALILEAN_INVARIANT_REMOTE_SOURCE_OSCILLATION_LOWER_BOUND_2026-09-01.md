## 1. Purpose

M5-433 derives for a source annulus at radius `R` feeding a target natural scale `r`

\[
|S_R(x_*)|\lesssim R^{-5/2}\|u\|_{L^2(A_R^+)}.
\]

This was sufficient for the fifth-root energy-atom route.

However, using raw `u` is not optimal for the later critical-packing problem. A velocity field that is nearly constant on `A_R^+` may have large local `L2` mass but a constant velocity contributes no strain. Moreover, multiplying such a field by a cutoff and applying a Bogovskii correction creates an artificial natural-frequency packet at frequency `R^{-1}`.

Therefore the later conditional `dot H^(1/2)` packet packing in the first version of M5-435 must not be justified from raw cutoff packets.

The correct quantity is velocity modulo constants.

## 2. Smooth annular strain localization

Let `eta_R` be a fixed-shape smooth annular cutoff supported where

\[
c_0R<|x-x_*|<c_3R
\]

and equal to one on the retained source annulus.

Write one strain component generated by this annulus schematically as

\[
S_R(x_*)
=
\int \eta_R(y)K(y)\omega(x_*+y)\,dy,
\]

where `K` is the homogeneous Biot--Savart strain kernel of degree `-3`.

Using

\[
\omega=\nabla\times u
\]

and integrating by parts away from the singular point gives

\[
\boxed{
S_R(x_*)
=
\int G_R(y)u(x_*+y)\,dy,
}
\]

where `G_R` is supported in the same fixed enlarged annular region and has the scaling

\[
\boxed{
\|G_R\|_2\le C R^{-5/2}.
}
\]

---

## 3. The integrated kernel has zero mean

The tensor `G_R` is a curl/derivative of a compactly supported smooth tensor field away from the target point. Hence componentwise

\[
\boxed{
\int_{\mathbb R^3}G_R(y)\,dy=0.
}
\]

Therefore for every constant vector `c in R^3`,

\[
\boxed{
S_R(x_*)
=
\int G_R(y)\,[u(x_*+y)-c]\,dy.
}
\]

This is the correct Galilean-invariant form of the far-energy estimate.

A constant velocity, or the constant part of a very low-frequency mode, is exactly invisible to the strain source.

## 4. Relative L2 far-strain estimate

Cauchy--Schwarz gives for every constant `c`

\[
|S_R(x_*)|
\le
C R^{-5/2}
\|u-c\|_{L^2(A_R^+)}.
\]

Taking the infimum,

\[
\boxed{
|S_R(x_*)|
\le
C R^{-5/2}
\inf_{c\in\mathbb R^3}
\|u-c\|_{L^2(A_R^+)}.
}
\]

Let `c_R` be the average of `u` over the fixed-shape enlarged annulus. Up to a domain-dependent constant, it realizes the minimizing constant, so the same estimate can be written with `u-c_R`.

## 5. Fixed-fraction source forces oscillatory energy

Assume the annulus supplies a fixed fraction `theta_s>0` of the target natural strain:

\[
|S_R(x_*)|
\ge
\theta_s\frac{\nu}{r^2}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\inf_c
\|u-c\|_{L^2(A_R^+)}
\ge
c_s\nu R^{5/2}r^{-2}.
}
\]

Squaring,

\[
\boxed{
\inf_c
\int_{A_R^+}|u-c|^2dx
\ge
c_s^2\nu^2\frac{R^5}{r^4}.
}
\]

This is strictly stronger information for the source mechanism than the raw kinetic-energy lower bound: the energy that pays the strain cannot be hidden in a locally constant Galilean drift.

## 7. Why the original cutoff critical-packet shortcut is unsafe

Let `u` be nearly constant on a large annulus. The field

\[
\chi_Ru
\]

has Fourier content at frequency `~R^{-1}` solely because of the cutoff. The Bogovskii correction required to make it divergence free is also at that frequency.

Thus

\[
P_R(\chi_Ru-b_R)
\]

can be nonzero even when the physical local velocity gradient is arbitrarily small.

Consequently one must not infer

\[
\|P_R(\chi_Ru-b_R)\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}
\lesssim
\text{a genuine piece of }\|u\|_{\dot H^{1/2}}
\]

without a cancellation/quotient argument.

This is exactly why M5-435 demoted the cumulative critical packing statement.
