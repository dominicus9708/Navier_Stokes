## 3. Three WKB regimes

Compare the exponents

\[
-\alpha-1
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
-2\alpha.
\]

### `alpha>1`

Then

\[
-2\alpha<-\alpha-1,
\]

so viscosity is strictly more singular:

\[
4\nu z^2Z_{zz}
\gg Z_z.
\]

There is no other term at the same `z^-2alpha` normal order capable of canceling the positive `c^2 alpha^2` principal coefficient.  Thus no decaying WKB branch exists.

### `0<alpha<1`

Then

\[
-\alpha-1<-2\alpha,
\]

so `Z_z` is strictly more singular than viscosity.

Again no other term reaches that normal order.  A nonzero decaying branch is impossible.

### `alpha=1`

The two principal normal orders coincide.

Using

\[
Z_z\sim cz^{-2}Z,
\qquad
z^2Z_{zz}\sim c^2z^{-2}Z,
\]

the leading coefficient is

\[
\boxed{
c+4\nu c^2=c(1+4\nu c).}
\]

The characteristic roots are

\[
c=0
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
c=-\frac1{4\nu}.
\]

The nonzero root corresponds to

\[
\exp\!\left(+\frac1{4\nu z}\right),
\]

not to a decaying flat branch.

Therefore no `c>0` branch exists.

## 4. Why common-tail transport is lower normal order

The linearization of the nonlinear Fuchsian term around the shared leading tail contains terms schematically of the form

\[
H_0\,DZ,
\qquad
Z\,DH_0,
\qquad
H_{0,\tau}\cdot\nabla_{S^2}Z,
\qquad
Z_\tau\cdot\nabla_{S^2}H_0.
\]

For a WKB-flat `Z`,

\[
DZ=(\partial_\eta-2z\partial_z)Z
\sim
-2c\alpha z^{-\alpha}Z.
\]

Thus transport reaches at worst

\[
z^{-\alpha}Z,
\]

which is one normal power weaker than

\[
Z_z\sim z^{-\alpha-1}Z
\]

and, at `alpha=1`, also weaker than the balanced `z^-2` viscous term.

Hence the large critical tail background does not alter the leading WKB characteristic equation.

The source difference is schematically linear in one derivative of `Z` times the shared tail/background plus stronger terms.  At WKB scale this is at most

\[

Solving the pressure Poisson equation does not introduce the extra `z^-1` needed to compete with the principal

\quad\text{or}\quad

Thus the pressure channel is subprincipal in the WKB characteristic calculation.

This is a principal-order statement only; it is not a global pressure estimate.
