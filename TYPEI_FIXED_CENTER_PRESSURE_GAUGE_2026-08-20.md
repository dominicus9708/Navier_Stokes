This note resolves the artificial affine-pressure issue created by the accelerating moving weighted-mean frame. ## 1. Why the accelerating frame creates an affine pressure

Let `X(t)` be a time-dependent center, `c=Xdot`, and

\[
v(y,t)=u(X(t)+y,t)-c(t).
\]

Then

\[
\partial_t v+(v\cdot\nabla)v
=-\nabla p+\nu\Delta v-c'(t).
\]

Absorbing the uniform force into pressure gives

\[
\widetilde p=p+c'(t)\cdot y.
\]

Thus a time-dependent translation creates an affine pressure term. This is harmless for the moving weighted-variance ledger because the center is chosen so that the weighted relative mean vanishes, but it is undesirable in the standard Type-I pressure quantity `D`, which normally subtracts only spatial constants.

## 2. Non-T center nesting supplies a fixed singular center

Let

\[
r_j=W_j^{-1/2},
\qquad
\mathfrak T_j=\frac{|X_{j+1}-X_j|}{r_j}.
\]

On the non-turnover branch assume

\[
\mathfrak T_j\le C_T.
\]

Since `r_{j+k}=q^{-k/2}r_j`, the center increments are summable:

\[
|X_{j+m}-X_j|
\le C_T\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}r_{j+k}
\le \frac{C_T}{1-q^{-1/2}}r_j.
\]

Hence there is a limiting point `X_*` with

\[
\boxed{|X_*-X_j|\lesssim r_j.}
\]

Thus every late natural core remains within bounded normalized distance of one fixed physical point.

## 3. Use two frames for two different jobs

### Ledger frame

Use the accelerating weighted-mean frame to derive local variance and turnover identities. In this frame the uniform acceleration term is either absorbed into an affine pressure or cancels in the weighted mean-energy identity.

### Compactness frame

For blow-up compactness, rescale directly around the fixed point `X_*`:

\[
U_j(y,s)=r_j u(X_*+r_jy,t_j+r_j^2s),
\]

\[
P_j(y,s)=r_j^2 p(X_*+r_jy,t_j+r_j^2s).
\]

This is the ordinary Navier--Stokes scaling about a fixed center and introduces **no acceleration and no artificial affine pressure**.

The first-hitting core center appears at

\[
y_j=\frac{X_j-X_*}{r_j},
\]

and the nesting estimate gives

\[
\boxed{|y_j|\lesssim1.}
\]

After a subsequence, `y_j -> y_infty`, so nontriviality may be tracked at a bounded point in the fixed-center coordinates.

## 4. Pressure decomposition in the fixed-center frame

In the fixed-center frame the pressure satisfies the standard Poisson equation

\[
-\Delta P_j=\partial_a\partial_b(U_{j,a}U_{j,b}).
\]

On a local ball one may decompose pressure into a near Calderon--Zygmund part generated by `U_j \otimes U_j` and a harmonic remote part. The previously derived affine-free remote-pressure estimate can be interpreted as controlling the genuinely non-affine harmonic remainder; no acceleration-generated affine term is present in this frame.

Therefore the remaining Type-I pressure task is genuine: bound the standard local pressure oscillation generated by the Type-I velocity tower and remote harmonic remainder. It is no longer contaminated by the arbitrary acceleration of the ledger frame.
