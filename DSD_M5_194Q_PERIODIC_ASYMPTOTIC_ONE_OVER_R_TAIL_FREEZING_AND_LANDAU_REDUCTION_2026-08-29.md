---

## 1. Purpose

M5-194P identified the hardest periodic tail as one which becomes asymptotically spatially `(-1)`-homogeneous, so that the similarity homogeneity defect tends to zero and the critical tail need not pay positive material export.

The present audit asks:

> Can the leading `1/r` angular profile itself remain genuinely periodic in similarity time?

Under a sufficiently strong asymptotic expansion and derivative control, the answer is **no**.

The reason is a direct mismatch of radial homogeneities in the Leray equation.

---

## 2. Periodic Leray equation

Use

\[
V_s-\Delta V
+\frac12V
+\frac12(Y\cdot\nabla)V
+(V\cdot\nabla)V
+\nabla P=0,
\]

\[
\nabla\cdot V=0.
\]

Assume `V` is periodic in `s` and, as `r=|Y| -> infinity`, has a leading critical expansion

\[
\boxed{
V(Y,s)
=
\frac1r\Phi(\theta,s)
+o(r^{-1}),
}
\]

uniformly in the periodic phase, with enough derivative control that

\[
\nabla^k
\left(
V-r^{-1}\Phi
\right)
=o(r^{-1-k})
\]

for the derivatives needed below.

This is stronger than a mere pointwise upper bound `|V| <= C/r`; it is an asymptotic-profile assumption.

---

## 3. Scaling terms cancel at exact degree `-1`

Let

\[
V_0(Y,s)=r^{-1}\Phi(\theta,s).
\]

Euler's homogeneous identity gives

\[
(Y\cdot\nabla)V_0=-V_0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac12V_0
+\frac12(Y\cdot\nabla)V_0=0.
}
\]

So the two linear similarity scaling terms cancel exactly on the leading critical tail.

---

## 4. Radial-order ledger

For the leading term,

\[
\boxed{(V_0)_s=r^{-1}\partial_s\Phi.}
\]

Spatial derivatives lower degree by one:

\[
\nabla V_0=O(r^{-2}),
\]

\[
\Delta V_0=O(r^{-3}).
\]

The convective term has the same stationary critical degree:

\[
(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0=O(r^{-3}).
\]

Since

\[
-\Delta P
=\partial_i\partial_j(V_iV_j),
\]

the source generated by `V_0` is order

\[
r^{-4}.
\]

The canonical pressure contribution associated with the critical tail is therefore

\[
P_0=O(r^{-2}),
\qquad
\nabla P_0=O(r^{-3}).
\]

Hence the leading radial-order ledger is

\[
\boxed{
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{term}&\text{order}\\
\hline
V_s&r^{-1}\\
\frac12V+\frac12Y\cdot\nabla V&0\text{ at the exact leading degree}\\
-\Delta V&r^{-3}\\
(V\cdot\nabla)V&r^{-3}\\
\nabla P&r^{-3}
\end{array}
}
\]

There is no other canonical `r^-1` term available to balance a nonzero `partial_s Phi`.

---

## 5. Leading tail must freeze

Multiply the Leray equation by `r` and let `r -> infinity` under the stated asymptotic derivative control.

All `r^-3` spatial terms vanish after multiplication by `r`.

The leading equation is therefore

\[
\boxed{
\partial_s\Phi(\theta,s)=0.
}
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\Phi(\theta,s)=\Phi(\theta)
}
\]

is independent of similarity time.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{periodic profile}
+\text{strong asymptotic }1/r\text{ expansion}
\Longrightarrow
\text{stationary leading critical halo}.
}
\]

The periodicity may survive only in lower-order terms or in the bounded core.

---

## 6. Pressure-harmonic caveat

Could an additional harmonic pressure produce an `r^-1` gradient and cancel `V_s`?

To produce `|nabla P|~r^-1`, pressure would need a nonconstant degree-zero leading component.

For a smooth angular function `p_0(theta)`,

\[
\Delta p_0(\theta)
=r^{-2}\Delta_{S^2}p_0.
\]

A degree-zero harmonic function on `R^3\setminus\{0\}` that is smooth on the full sphere must satisfy

\[
\Delta_{S^2}p_0=0,
\]

hence be constant on `S^2`.

Its gradient is zero.

Therefore the ordinary smooth full-sphere pressure gauge does not supply a nontrivial `r^-1` cancellation channel.

More singular/noncanonical harmonic pressure behavior would have to be stated as a separate failure of the pressure/asymptotic class and is not silently included.

---

## 7. Next order is stationary Navier--Stokes

Once

\[
\partial_s\Phi=0,
\]

the leading `r^-1` field is stationary.

At order `r^-3`, the Leray scaling terms still cancel because the leading velocity has degree `-1`.

Therefore the coefficient system is exactly the stationary homogeneous Navier--Stokes equation for

\[
V_0(Y)=\frac1r\Phi(\theta):
\]

\[
\boxed{
-\Delta V_0
+(V_0\cdot\nabla)V_0
+\nabla P_0=0,
\qquad
\nabla\cdot V_0=0.
}
\]

Thus the sphere profile obeys the same system audited in M5-194H.

---

## 8. Šverák classification applies to the leading coefficient

Under the smooth-full-sphere assumptions of the stationary `(-1)`-homogeneous classification,

\[
\boxed{
V_0=0
\quad\text{or}\quad
V_0\text{ is a Landau profile}.
}
\]

In particular, the favorable nonzero purely tangential spherical-Killing profile is not an allowed exact stationary NS coefficient.

Therefore a strongly asymptotically homogeneous periodic DSS survivor has only two leading critical-tail possibilities:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi=0
\quad\lor\quad
\Phi=\Phi_{Landau}.
}
\]

---

## 9. If the leading coefficient is zero

If

\[
\Phi=0,
\]

then

\[
V=o(r^{-1}).
\]

This is strictly better than the borderline Type-I upper rate at the leading-order level.

Whether it is enough to force global `L^3` depends on the quantitative remainder rate.

For example, if

\[
|V|\lesssim r^{-1-\epsilon}
\]

for any `epsilon>0`, then

\[
\int_{|Y|>1}|V|^3dY<\infty,
\]

and the periodic `L^3` Liouville theorem closes the branch.

But the weaker statement

\[
V=o(r^{-1})
\]

alone need not be integrable without a quantified rate.

Thus a zero leading coefficient leaves a **subleading critical-log tail** possibility unless improved decay is proved.

---

## 10. Landau-leading periodic halo

If

\[
\Phi=\Phi_{Landau}\ne0,
\]

then the periodic profile has asymptotic form

\[
\boxed{
V(Y,s)
=
V_{Landau}(Y)
+o(r^{-1}),
}
\]

with a stationary leading halo and time dependence only below the leading critical order.

This is now a much narrower object than an arbitrary periodic critical tail.

Landau profiles solve the stationary Navier--Stokes equations on `R^3\setminus\{0\}` and carry a point-force defect when extended distributionally through the origin.

However, using that point-force fact to exclude a **far-field Landau coefficient of a globally smooth time-periodic Leray profile** requires an additional stress/momentum-flux argument at spatial infinity. It is not automatic from the local classification itself.

---

## 11. DSD verdict

### PROVED UNDER THE STRONG ASYMPTOTIC-PROFILE HYPOTHESIS

- a time-dependent leading `1/r` coefficient is impossible;
- the leading coefficient is stationary;
- the stationary leading coefficient satisfies the exact `(-1)`-homogeneous NS sphere system;
- it is `0` or Landau under the smooth-sphere classification.

### NOT PROVED FROM A MERE TYPE-I UPPER BOUND

The estimate

\[
|V(Y,s)|\le C/(1+|Y|)
\]

alone does not produce a convergent angular coefficient

\[
rV(r\theta,s)\to\Phi(\theta,s).
\]

Thus the asymptotic-profile compactness is a new explicit gate.

### REMAINING PERIODIC TAILS

1. no asymptotic `rV` profile due to angular/log-radial tail wandering;
2. zero leading profile but only unquantified `o(1/r)` decay;
3. stationary Landau leading coefficient plus lower-order periodic corrections.

## 12. Next audit target

The next highest-value question is the **Landau-leading flux coefficient**.

For a periodic Leray solution smooth on all of `R^3`, derive the period-averaged momentum/stress balance on a large sphere and determine whether the coefficient of a Landau `1/r` far field must vanish in the absence of external force.

If the large-sphere stress flux is forced to zero, the Landau coefficient disappears, leaving only the zero-leading/subcritical-tail branch.

If not, the exact conserved/oscillatory quantity supporting a nonzero Landau far field becomes the next explicit obstruction.
