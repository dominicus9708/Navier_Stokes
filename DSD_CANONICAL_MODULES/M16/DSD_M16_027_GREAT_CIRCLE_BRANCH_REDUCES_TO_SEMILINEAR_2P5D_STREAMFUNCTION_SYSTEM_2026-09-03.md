# DSD M16-027 — Great-circle branch reduces to a semilinear 2.5D streamfunction system

Date: 2026-09-03
Canonical ID: **M16-027**

Status: **INTERNAL LOWER-DIMENSIONAL STRUCTURAL REDUCTION / THE RANK-ONE GREAT-CIRCLE BRANCH OF M16-026, AFTER SPATIAL ANALYTICITY EXTENDS A FIXED VORTICITY-PLANE CONSTRAINT `n dot W = 0`, ADMITS A GLOBAL/LOCAL HORIZONTAL STREAMFUNCTION. THE LAPLACIAN EIGENLINE THEN FORCES `grad_h Delta q` TO BE PARALLEL TO `grad_h q`, SO LOCALLY `Delta q = F(q,x_3)` AND `kappa = partial_q F`. THE STRAIN EIGENLINE SIMULTANEOUSLY FORCES THE VELOCITY COMPONENT NORMAL TO THE VORTICITY PLANE TO BE CONSTANT ON THE SAME `q`-LEVEL CURVES. THIS IS A 2.5D SEMILINEAR REDUCTION, NOT YET A FULL TWO-DIMENSIONALIZATION / GLOBAL REGULARITY REMAINS UNPROVED.**

---

## 1. Fix the great-circle plane

M16-026 gives, on the rank-one branch and after spatial analyticity continuation at a fixed time,

\[
\boxed{n\cdot W=0}
\]

for one fixed unit vector `n`.

Choose coordinates with

\[
n=e_3.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{W=(W_1,W_2,0).}
\]

Since

\[
\nabla\cdot W=0,
\]

we have

\[
\partial_1W_1+\partial_2W_2=0.
\]

---

## 2. Horizontal streamfunction

On each simply connected horizontal slice (or locally in the active region), there exists a scalar streamfunction `q` such that

\[
\boxed{
W_h=J\nabla_h q
=(\partial_2q,-\partial_1q).
}
\]

Here

\[
\nabla_h=(\partial_1,\partial_2)
\]

and `J` denotes the ninety-degree rotation in the horizontal plane.

The additive normalization of `q` may depend on `x_3`; it is irrelevant to the local identities below.

---

## 3. Laplacian eigenline becomes semilinear streamfunction geometry

CE-H gives

\[
\Delta W=\kappa W.
\]

Because horizontal derivatives commute with the full three-dimensional Laplacian,

\[
\Delta W_h
=J\nabla_h(\Delta q).
\]

Therefore

\[
J\nabla_h(\Delta q)
=\kappa J\nabla_hq.
\]

Multiplying by `J^{-1}`,

\[
\boxed{
\nabla_h(\Delta q)
=\kappa\nabla_h q.
}
\]

Consequences:

1. `Delta q` is constant along connected horizontal level curves of `q`;
2. `kappa` is the derivative of that level-set relation.

On any regular region where

\[
\nabla_hq\neq0,
\]

there exists a local function `F` such that

\[
\boxed{
\Delta q=F(q,x_3),
}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
\kappa=\partial_qF(q,x_3).
}
\]

This is compatible with the general CE-H first-integral law

\[
W\cdot\nabla\kappa=0,
\]

because `W_h=J grad_h q` is tangent to the `q`-level curves.

---

## 4. Velocity structure from `W_3=0`

Write

\[
U=(U_h,U_3).
\]

Since

\[
W_3=\partial_1U_2-\partial_2U_1=0,
\]

the horizontal velocity is locally curl-free:

\[
\boxed{U_h=\nabla_h\phi}
\]

for a scalar potential `phi`.

The horizontal vorticity components are then

\[
W_1=\partial_2U_3-\partial_3\partial_2\phi,
\]

\[
W_2=\partial_3\partial_1\phi-\partial_1U_3.
\]

Therefore, after choosing the sign convention of Section 2,

\[
\boxed{
q=U_3-\partial_3\phi
}
\]

up to an `x_3`-dependent additive gauge.

---

## 5. Strain eigenline forces `U_3` to be constant on q-level curves

The CE-H strain eigenline is equivalent to

\[
(W\cdot\nabla)U=\sigma W.
\]

Its third component gives

\[
(W_h\cdot\nabla_h)U_3=0.
\]

Since

\[
W_h=J\nabla_hq
\]

is tangent to the horizontal level curves of `q`, we obtain

\[
\boxed{
J\nabla_hq\cdot\nabla_hU_3=0.
}
\]

Hence on every regular connected region,

\[
\boxed{
U_3=G(q,x_3)
}
\]

for some local scalar `G`.

Thus both

\[
\Delta q
\]

and

\[
U_3
\]

are functions of the same horizontal streamfunction label `q`, with `x_3` acting as a parameter.

---

## 6. Horizontal strain eigenvector condition

Because

\[
U_h=\nabla_h\phi,
\]

the horizontal part of

\[
(W\cdot\nabla)U=\sigma W
\]

becomes

\[
\boxed{
(\nabla_h^2\phi)(J\nabla_hq)
=\sigma(J\nabla_hq).
}
\]

Therefore the tangent direction to the `q`-level curves is an eigenvector of the horizontal Hessian of `phi`.

The rank-one great-circle survivor is thus not an arbitrary one-component-vorticity flow; it simultaneously satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
W_h&=J\nabla_hq,\\
\Delta q&=F(q,x_3),\\
\kappa&=F_q(q,x_3),\\
U_3&=G(q,x_3),\\
(\nabla_h^2\phi)J\nabla_hq&=\sigma J\nabla_hq,\\
\Delta_h\phi+\partial_3U_3&=0.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

This is the canonical **semilinear 2.5D CE-H system**.

---

## 7. Audit of possible shortcut

The condition

\[
W_3=0
\]

alone does **not** imply that the three-dimensional velocity is two-dimensional.

Indeed `U_3` and `phi` may both depend on `x_3`, and horizontal vorticity can be generated by those derivatives.

Therefore it would be incorrect to declare the rank-one branch regular merely from the vanishing of one vorticity component.

The external regularity literature contains many sufficient criteria involving one velocity/gradient component or two vorticity components, but no directly matching unconditional theorem was identified here that turns the exact condition `W_3=0` plus the present Type-I bounds into an immediate closure.

The additional semilinear CE-H relations above are therefore essential.
