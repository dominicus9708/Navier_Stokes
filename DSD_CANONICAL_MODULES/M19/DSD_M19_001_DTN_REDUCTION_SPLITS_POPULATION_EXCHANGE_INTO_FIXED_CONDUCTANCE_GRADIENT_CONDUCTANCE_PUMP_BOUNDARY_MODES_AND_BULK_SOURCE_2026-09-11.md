## 2. Controlled two-population connector

Let \(C_{ij}(\theta)\) be a controlled connector collar between persistent populations \(P_i\) and \(P_j\).

After pullback to a fixed reference connector \(C_{ij}\), split its boundary into

\[
\partial C_{ij}
=
\Gamma_i\cup\Gamma_j\cup\Gamma_0,
\]

where \(\Gamma_i\) and \(\Gamma_j\) face the two populations and \(\Gamma_0\) is the lateral collar boundary.

The pulled-back elliptic operator may be written abstractly as

\[
L_\theta v
:=-\nabla\cdot(A_\theta\nabla v),
\]

with uniformly elliptic symmetric \(A_\theta\) on the controlled geometry branch.

The isotropic case is \(A_\theta=I\).

For the actual amplitude potential \(u\), define the exact bulk forcing

\[
\boxed{f_\theta:=L_\theta u.}
\]

This is only a definition. No new PDE assumption is introduced.

## 3. Constant-mode conductance

Let \(h_{ij,\theta}\) solve

\[
L_\theta h=0
\]

with

\[
h=1\quad\text{on }\Gamma_i,
\qquad
h=0\quad\text{on }\Gamma_j,
\qquad
(A_\theta\nabla h)\cdot n=0
\quad\text{on }\Gamma_0.
\]

Define the connector capacity/conductance

\[
\boxed{
G_{ij}(\theta)
:=
\int_{C_{ij}}
\nabla h_{ij,\theta}\cdot A_\theta\nabla h_{ij,\theta}\,dx
>0.
}
\]

By symmetry of the elliptic form,

\[
\boxed{G_{ij}=G_{ji}.}
\]

Uniform connector geometry and ellipticity give, on the controlled compact branch,

\[
0<G_-\le G_{ij}(\theta)\le G_+<\infty.
\]

## 4. Choose one node potential per population

## 5. Exact connector decomposition

By linearity, decompose

\[
\boxed{u=u^{const}+u^{mode}+u^{src}.}
\]

The constant component is the homogeneous connector solution with constant endpoint data:

\[
 u^{const}
 =V_j+(V_i-V_j)h_{ij}.
\]

The mode component is homogeneous,

\[
L_\theta u^{mode}=0,
\]

and carries the remaining nonconstant boundary traces after subtracting \(V_i,V_j\).

The source component solves

\[
L_\theta u^{src}=f_\theta
\]

with zero endpoint Dirichlet data and the corresponding homogeneous lateral condition.

Therefore the oriented integrated connector current splits exactly as

\[
\boxed{
 j_{ij}
 =
 j_{ij}^{cond}
 +r_{ij}^{mode}
 +r_{ij}^{src}.
}
\]

With orientation chosen from \(i\) to \(j\),

\[
\boxed{
 j_{ij}^{cond}
 =G_{ij}(V_i-V_j).
}
\]

The two residuals are the integrated normal fluxes generated by \(u^{mode}\) and \(u^{src}\).

Thus

\[
\boxed{
 j_{ij}
 =G_{ij}(V_i-V_j)
 +r_{ij}^{mode}
 +r_{ij}^{src}.
}
\]

This is an exact linear connector identity once the connector, averaging rule, and boundary conditions have been fixed.

## 6. Finite graph form

Orient the finite persistent-population graph once and let \(B\) be its incidence matrix.

Let

\[
V=(V_1,\dots,V_N)^T,

and let \(G(\theta)\) be the positive diagonal matrix of edge conductances.

Then

\[
\boxed{
 j(\theta)

The sign convention for \(B\) may reverse the first term globally; none of the conclusions below depend on that convention.

## 7. Fixed conductance: pure gradient current cannot support a conservative cycle

First suppose

\[
G(\theta)\equiv G_0
\]

is fixed and

\[
r^{mode}=r^{src}=0.
\]

Then

\[
 j=G_0B^TV.
\]

If the current is conservative on the finite graph,

\[
Bj=0,
\]

then

\[
0
=V^TBj
=(B^TV)^TG_0(B^TV).
\]

Because \(G_0>0\),

\[
B^TV=0.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{j=0.}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{nonzero conservative cycle current}
\not\subset
\text{fixed symmetric conductance gradient alone}.
}
\]

This closes the naive finite-gradient-cycle possibility.

## 8. Time-dependent conductance produces a genuine pump defect

The controlled connector geometry may vary in material time, so \(G(\theta)\) need not be fixed.

A nonzero long-time mean circulation cannot therefore be ruled out merely by Section 7.

Take an invariant/long-time mean and write

\[
\bar G:=\langle G\rangle,
\qquad
\bar V:=\langle V\rangle.
\]

Then exactly

\[
\begin{aligned}
\langle GB^TV\rangle
&=
\bar G B^T\bar V
+
\left\langle
(G-\bar G)B^T(V-\bar V)
\right\rangle.
\end{aligned}
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
r^{pump}
:=
\left\langle
(G-\bar G)B^T(V-\bar V)
\right\rangle.
}
\]

Thus the mean current obeys

\[
\boxed{
\bar j
=
\bar G B^T\bar V
+r^{pump}
+\bar r^{mode}
+\bar r^{src}.
}
\]

The first term is an ordinary fixed-conductance graph gradient.

The second is a conductance--potential covariance. It is the exact coarse-network analogue of time-dependent metric/geometry pumping.
