## 1. Improvement over M19-233

M19-233 proves that an escaping cavity unit mode cannot keep a positive fraction of its fixed vorticity currency at radii

\[
1\ll r\ll R_j.
\]

That result still allows vorticity at any fixed fractional radius \(r=cR_j\), \(0<c<1\).

A better weight removes this remaining freedom.

The key is to choose a radial vorticity multiplier that

1. vanishes at the cavity boundary;
2. has vanishing normal derivative there, so diffusion produces no boundary enstrophy term;
3. preserves a strictly negative similarity-vorticity sign throughout the interior.

## 2. Double-zero weight

Set

\[
s_j(y):=\frac{|y|^2}{R_j^2}
\]

and define

\[
\boxed{
a_j(y):=(1-s_j(y))^2.}
\]

Then on \(B_{R_j}\),

\[
a_j\ge0,
\qquad
a_j|_{S_{R_j}}=0,
\qquad
\partial_na_j|_{S_{R_j}}=0.
\]

Hence both the direct diffusion boundary term and the integration-by-parts boundary term generated by \(\nabla a_j\) vanish.

The radial derivatives are explicit:

\[
\boxed{
y\cdot\nabla a_j=-4s_j(1-s_j),}
\]

and in three dimensions

\[
\boxed{
\Delta a_j
=\frac{-12+20s_j}{R_j^2}.
}
\]

Thus

\[
\|\Delta a_j\|_\infty=O(R_j^{-2}),
\qquad
\|\nabla a_j\|_\infty=O(R_j^{-1}).
\]

## 3. Similarity term becomes strictly coercive

Use the exact weighted vorticity identity from M19-233.

The amplitude and dilation contribution is

\[
-\frac14a_j|\eta_j|^2
+\frac14(y\cdot\nabla a_j)|\eta_j|^2.
\]

Substituting the explicit weight gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
-\frac14a_j
+\frac14y\cdot\nabla a_j
&=-\frac14(1-s_j)^2-s_j(1-s_j)\\
&=-\frac14(1-s_j)(1+3s_j).
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
-\frac14a_j
+\frac14y\cdot\nabla a_j
=-\frac14(1-s_j)(1+3s_j)<0
}
\]

at every interior point \(|y|<R_j\).

Unlike the slowly varying cutoff in M19-233, no similarity-flux error has to be made small: the drift itself strengthens the negative sign.

## 4. Period-integrated identity

Because \(a_j\) is radial, the relative rotation preserves the weighted vorticity norm at the period endpoints.

The period derivative therefore vanishes. Using the exact M19-233 identity and moving the coercive terms to the left gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\nu\int_0^{S_j}\int a_j|\nabla\eta_j|^2
+\frac14\int_0^{S_j}\int(1-s_j)(1+3s_j)|\eta_j|^2\\
&\qquad=
\frac\nu2\int_0^{S_j}\int(\Delta a_j)|\eta_j|^2
+\frac12\int_0^{S_j}\int(U_j\cdot\nabla a_j)|\eta_j|^2\\
&\qquad\quad+
\int_0^{S_j}\int a_j\eta_j^TS_{U_j}\eta_j
-\int_0^{S_j}\int a_j\eta_j\cdot((w_j\cdot\nabla)\Omega_j)\\
&\qquad\quad+
\int_0^{S_j}\int a_j\eta_j\cdot((\Omega_j\cdot\nabla)w_j).
\end{aligned}}
\]

There is **no boundary vorticity term** because \(a_j\) has a double zero at \(S_{R_j}\).

## 6. Relative-boundary localization

Discard the first nonnegative term on the left. We obtain

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^{S_j}\int_{B_{R_j}}
(1-s_j)(1+3s_j)|\eta_j|^2\,dy\,ds
\to0.
}
\]

Fix any \(0<\delta<1\). On

\[
|y|\le(1-\delta)R_j,
\]

we have

\[
s_j\le(1-\delta)^2
\]

and therefore

\[
(1-s_j)(1+3s_j)
\ge
1-(1-\delta)^2
=:c_\delta>0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^{S_j}\int_{|y|\le(1-\delta)R_j}
|\eta_j|^2\,dy\,ds
\to0
\qquad
\forall\delta>0.
}
\]

Since M19-232 gives the total limit

\[
\int_0^{S_j}\|\eta_j\|_2^2ds

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^{S_j}\int_{(1-\delta)R_j<|y|<R_j}
