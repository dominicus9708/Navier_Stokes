## 2. Pressure source at order n

The pressure equation is

\[
-\Delta P
=
\partial_i\partial_j(U_iU_j).
\]

At pressure level `n`, the quadratic stress is built from pairs

\[
a+b=n,

\[
T^{(n)}_{ij}

\sum_{a+b=n}
B_{a,i}B_{b,j}

\[
\boxed{

Two derivatives give

\[
\boxed{

## 3. Zero-q radial resonance

For a zero-q-frequency pressure mode

\[

=
r^{-(2n+2)}Y_l(\omega),
\]

use

\[
\Delta(r^sY_l)
=
r^{s-2}\left[s(s+1)-l(l+1)\right]Y_l.
\]

Set

\[
s=-(2n+2).
\]

Then

\[
s(s+1)
=(2n+2)(2n+1).
\]

Hence radial harmonic resonance occurs precisely when

\[
l(l+1)
=(2n+2)(2n+1),
\]

namely

\[
\boxed{l=2n+1.}
\]

For nonzero real q-frequency the q-derivative shifts the indicial factor off this real zero, so the dangerous resonance is the q-average / Bohr-zero mode.

Let

\[

be a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree `2n+1`.

The q-averaged stress has homogeneity

\[
\overline T^{(n)}\sim r^{-(2n+2)}.

On an annulus, both boundary terms produced by two integrations by parts are scale invariant and cancel between the two radial boundaries.
Thus the resonant source moment is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{

\left[D^2H_{2n+1}\right]_{ij}\big|_{r=1}

\]

In other words, the no-log condition can be written as a harmonic stress-moment constraint.
