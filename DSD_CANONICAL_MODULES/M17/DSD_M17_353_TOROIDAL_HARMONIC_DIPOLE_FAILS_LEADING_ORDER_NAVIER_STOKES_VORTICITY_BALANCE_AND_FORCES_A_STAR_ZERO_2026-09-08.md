## 2. Velocity generated by the dipole vorticity

Define

\[
\boxed{
V_{-1}(x)
:=
\frac12\left(
\frac{a_*}{r}
+
\frac{(a_*\cdot x)x}{r^3}
\right),
\qquad r=|x|.
}
\]

Direct differentiation gives

\[
\boxed{\nabla\cdot V_{-1}=0,}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{\nabla\times V_{-1}=\Omega_{-2}.}
\]

Thus `V_{-1}` is the unique decaying homogeneous degree `-1` velocity associated with the toroidal dipole, modulo a spatially constant Galilean velocity.

There is no additional nonzero decaying curl-free/divergence-free homogeneous degree `-1` field on the simply connected exterior: such a field would be the gradient of a harmonic homogeneous degree-zero scalar, and only constants occur at degree zero.

Hence after fixing the Galilean constant,

\[
\boxed{
V(x,t)=V_{-1}(x)+O(r^{-2}).
}
\]

## 3. Orders of the lower multipoles

Write

\[
\Omega
=
\Omega_{-2}+\Omega_{\le-3},
\qquad
V
=
V_{-1}+V_{\le-2},
\]

with

\[
\Omega_{\le-3}=O(r^{-3}),
\qquad
\nabla\Omega_{\le-3}=O(r^{-4}),
\]

and

\[
V_{\le-2}=O(r^{-2}),
\qquad
\nabla V_{\le-2}=O(r^{-3}).
\]

Therefore every nonlinear cross term involving at least one lower multipole is `O(r^-5)`:

\[
V_{-1}\cdot\nabla\Omega_{\le-3}=O(r^{-5}),
\]

\[
V_{\le-2}\cdot\nabla\Omega_{-2}=O(r^{-5}),
\]

\[
\Omega_{-2}\cdot\nabla V_{\le-2}=O(r^{-5}),
\]

\[
\Omega_{\le-3}\cdot\nabla V_{-1}=O(r^{-5}).
\]

Thus the entire `r^-4` nonlinear balance is determined solely by `(V_{-1},Omega_{-2})`.

## 4. Time and diffusion terms at the leading order

Because `a_*` is time independent,

\[
\partial_t\Omega_{-2}=0.
\]

The coherent harmonic exterior gives

\[
\Delta\Omega=0
\]

exactly for sufficiently large `r`, hence in particular

\[
\Delta\Omega_{-2}=0.
\]

Therefore the physical vorticity equation

\[
\partial_t\Omega
+(V\cdot\nabla)\Omega
-(\Omega\cdot\nabla)V
=
\Delta\Omega
\]

\[
\boxed{
(V_{-1}\cdot\nabla)\Omega_{-2}
-(\Omega_{-2}\cdot\nabla)V_{-1}
=0.
}
\]

## 5. Exact dipole self-interaction

For

\[
\Omega_{-2}
=
\frac{a_*\times x}{r^3},
\]

and

\[
V_{-1}
=
\frac12\left(
\frac{a_*}{r}
+
\frac{(a_*\cdot x)x}{r^3}
\right),
\]

direct vector differentiation yields

\[
\boxed{
(V_{-1}\cdot\nabla)\Omega_{-2}
-(\Omega_{-2}\cdot\nabla)V_{-1}
=
-3\frac{(a_*\cdot x)(a_*\times x)}{r^6}.
}
\]

For example, if `a_*=e_3`, the vector is

\[
\left(
\frac{3yz}{r^6},
-
\frac{3xz}{r^6},
0
\right),
\]

which is nonzero at generic points.

## 6. Nonzero a_* is impossible on the harmonic exterior

If

\[
a_*\ne0,
\]

choose `x` with both

\[
a_*\cdot x\ne0
\]

and

\[
a_*\times x\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
-3\frac{(a_*\cdot x)(a_*\times x)}{r^6}\ne0.
\]

This contradicts the required degree-`-4` Navier--Stokes vorticity balance.

Hence

\[
\boxed{a_*=0.}
\]

The unique harmonic weak-critical dipole obstruction is eliminated by the nonlinear PDE itself.
