## 2. Point-marker degeneration is not lineage loss

Let \(\Sigma(\theta)\) be a transported material surface and

\[
\Phi(\theta)=\int_{\Sigma(\theta)}W\cdot n\,dA
\]

its directed vorticity-flux label.

A material point \(X(\theta)\in\Sigma(\theta)\) may satisfy

\[
|W(X(\theta_j),\theta_j)|\to0
\]

while

\[
|\Phi(\theta_j)|\ge\phi_0>0.
\]

Diffusion may move the active amplitude maximum to a different location on the same material surface.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{marker amplitude degeneration}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{material-flux lineage replacement}.
}
\]

This is the direct scope firewall imported from M5-518.

## 3. Packet amplitude floor from flux is only a supremum statement

If

\[

|\Phi(\theta)|\ge\phi_0,
\]

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{\Sigma(\theta)}|W|
\ge\frac{\phi_0}{A_*}.

This gives a packet-level amplitude floor somewhere on the lineage.

It does **not** force the coefficient-active microcarrier point to have that amplitude.

Hence any ARG that uses fixed flux must also control the spatial relation between

1. the coefficient-active low-amplitude subset;
2. the high-amplitude representative guaranteed by flux/area;
3. the same transported material surface.

Without such a relation there is no amplitude return.
