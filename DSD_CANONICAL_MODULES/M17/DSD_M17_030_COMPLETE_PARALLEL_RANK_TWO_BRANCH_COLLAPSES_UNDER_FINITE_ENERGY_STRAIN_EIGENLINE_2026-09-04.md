## 2. Geodesic director implies curl xi is parallel to xi

For a unit vector field,

\[
(\xi\cdot\nabla)\xi
=\nabla\frac{|\xi|^2}{2}
-\xi\times(\nabla\times\xi).
\]

Since `|xi|=1` and `(xi·grad)xi=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\xi\times(\nabla\times\xi)=0.
}
\]

Therefore there is a scalar twist

\[
\boxed{
\tau:=\xi\cdot(\nabla\times\xi)
}
\]

such that

\[
\boxed{
\nabla\times\xi=\tau\xi.
}
\]

For the transverse `2 x 2` matrix `A`, `tau` is, up to the fixed orientation convention, twice its antisymmetric/twist coefficient.

The M17-029 strict negative discriminant condition cannot hold with zero antisymmetric part. Hence on the complete nonfocusing rank-two line,

\[
\boxed{\tau\ne0.}
\]

## 3. CE-H strain eigenline fixes the line derivative of velocity

CE-H gives

\[
\Sigma W=\sigma W.
\]

The antisymmetric part of `grad U` is generated by vorticity and annihilates `W` itself. Hence

\[
(\nabla U)W=\Sigma W=\sigma W.
\]

Since `W=rho xi` on the active set,

\[
\boxed{
(\xi\cdot\nabla)U=\sigma\xi.
}
\]

Along one complete straight vortex line, `xi=xi_0` is constant.
Decompose

\[
U=u\xi+V,
\qquad
V\cdot\xi=0.
\]

Then

\[
\frac{dU}{ds}
=u'\xi+V'
=\sigma\xi.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{V'=0,}
\qquad
\boxed{u'=\sigma.}
\]

Thus the transverse velocity component is constant along the entire straight line.

## 4. Finite-energy/decay removes the constant transverse velocity

The complete nonfocusing line has a local transverse separation factor

\[
\Delta_\perp(s)
=\det(I+sA_0)
\sim (\det A_0)s^2
\qquad(|s|\to\infty).
\]

Hence a nonzero constant transverse component `V` persists on a complete infinitesimal material line tube of infinite physical measure.

The retained whole-space class has finite enstrophy and the standard decay/finite-energy control. In particular, by the divergence-free Sobolev estimate one may use

\[
U\in L^6(\mathbb R^3)
\]

on the retained finite-enstrophy profile class.

A nonzero line-constant `V` on a positive transverse label patch would make the `L^6` integral diverge along the complete tube.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{V=0.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{U=u\xi}
\]

throughout the complete parallel rank-two component.

This step can equivalently be stated under the stronger explicit assumption `U->0` along the complete line.

## 5. Curl U = W forces grad u to be longitudinal

Use

\[
U=u\xi.
\]

Then

\[
\nabla\times U
=\nabla u\times\xi
+u\,\nabla\times\xi.
\]

Since

\[
\nabla\times U=W=\rho\xi
\]

and

\[
\nabla\times\xi=\tau\xi,
\]

we have

\[
\nabla u\times\xi
+u\tau\xi
=\rho\xi.
\]

The first term is perpendicular to `xi`, while the remaining terms are parallel to `xi`.
Hence

\[
\boxed{
\nabla u\times\xi=0.
}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\nabla u=a\xi
}
\]

for a scalar

\[
a=\xi\cdot\nabla u=u'=\sigma.
\]

The parallel component simultaneously gives

\[
\boxed{
\rho=u\tau.
}
\]

## 6. Twist obstructs a nonconstant scalar potential along xi

Because `grad u` is a gradient,

\[
\nabla\times(\nabla u)=0.
\]

But

\[
\nabla u=a\xi,
\]

so

\[
0
=\nabla\times(a\xi)
=\nabla a\times\xi
+a\,\nabla\times\xi.
\]

Dot with `xi`:

\[
0
=a\,\xi\cdot(\nabla\times\xi)
=a\tau.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{a\tau=0.}
\]

On the complete nonfocusing rank-two line,

\[
\tau\ne0,
\]

so

\[
\boxed{a=0.}
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{\nabla u=0.}
\]

and `u` is constant on the connected component.

## 7. Finite energy then forces the constant to vanish

A nonzero constant `u` gives

\[
|U|=|u|
\]

on a positive-volume complete line component, which is incompatible with the retained finite-energy/`L^6`/decay class.
Therefore

\[
\boxed{u=0.}
\]

Hence

\[
U=0
\]

on the component, and consequently

\[
\boxed{W=\nabla\times U=0.}
\]

This contradicts the active rank-two hypothesis

\[
\rho=|W|>0.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
R_2^{parallel,complete}
\Longrightarrow
\bot
}
\]

within the retained finite-energy/decay CE-H class.
