## 1. Pressure Poisson equation in the similarity normalization

From M17-044,

\[
D_BU
=\Delta U-\frac12U-\nabla P,
\qquad
B=U+\frac12y.
\]

Take divergence.
Since

\[
\nabla\cdot U=0,
\]

the only nonlinear contribution is

\[
\partial_iB_j\partial_jU_i
=\partial_iU_j\partial_jU_i.
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
-\Delta P
=\operatorname{tr}(A^2),
\qquad
A=\nabla U.
}
\]

Write

\[
A=\Sigma+\Omega.
\]

Then

\[
\operatorname{tr}(A^2)
=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma^2)
+\operatorname{tr}(\Omega^2).
\]

For three-dimensional vorticity,

\[
\operatorname{tr}(\Omega^2)
=-\frac12|W|^2.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
-\Delta P
=|\Sigma|^2-rac12\rho^2.
}
\]

Define the pressure source

\[
\boxed{
S_P:=|\Sigma|^2-rac12\rho^2.
}
\]

\[

\[

\boxed{

## 4. Near/far pressure split

Choose a retained core ball and a larger parent ball as in the earlier M5 pressure-screening analysis.
Split

\[
P=P_{near}+P_{far}.
\]

The near pressure is generated by `S_P` in/near the active parent region.

The far pressure is generated by sources outside the parent ball.
Inside the retained inner ball,

\[
\boxed{\Delta P_{far}=0.}
\]

Therefore `P_far` has a harmonic expansion

\[
P_{far}
=b_0+b_1\cdot x
+\pi_2+\pi_3+\pi_4+\cdots,
\]

where each `pi_m` is a homogeneous harmonic polynomial of degree `m` in the local expansion.

## 5. Which far pressure modes enter DSAIG?

DSAIG uses

\[
(p\cdot\nabla_h)\nabla_h^2P_{far}.
\]

Consider each harmonic degree.

### Degree 0

\[
P_{far}=b_0.
\]

Hessian zero.

### Degree 1

\[
P_{far}=b_1\cdot x.
\]

Hessian zero.

### Degree 2

\[
P_{far}=\pi_2.
\]

Its Hessian is a constant symmetric trace-free matrix, so

\[
\boxed{
(p\cdot\nabla_h)\nabla_h^2\pi_2=0.
}
\]

Thus the far pressure quadrupole affects the instantaneous strain evolution, as in M5-315, but **not** the DSAIG derivative-alignment forcing.

### Degree 3

For

\[
P_{far}=\pi_3,
\]

the Hessian is linear and its directional derivative is a nonzero constant third-order harmonic tensor in general.

Therefore degree three is the first far pressure mode visible to DSAIG.

## 10. Compactness bound versus exact cancellation

On the compact smooth hard hull, the relevant finite-order derivatives and near/far pressure operators are bounded on the retained core.

This prevents unbounded compensation from being hidden in an uncontrolled jet.

However boundedness does not forbid exact cancellation:

\[
P_{Q_0}^{\perp}V_p
=P_{Q_0}^{\perp}N_p.

A closure requires either

1. a sign/orthogonality theorem separating the two terms;
2. a moment theorem showing the far/near pressure cannot track the frozen material anisotropy line recurrently;
3. a symmetry reduction forced by persistent cancellation.

None is established here.
