## 2. Exact identity

For smooth solutions, write the vorticity equation as

\[
D_t\omega-\nu\Delta\omega=S\omega,
\qquad
D_t=\partial_t+u\cdot\nabla.
\]

Let `w(x,t)>0` be any smooth scalar weight. Multiplying by `w omega`, integrating, using `div u=0`, and integrating the Laplacian by parts gives

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\frac d{dt}\int w|\omega|^2
=
\int w\,\omega\cdot S\omega
-\nu\int w|\nabla\omega|^2
+\frac12\int (D_tw+\nu\Delta w)|\omega|^2.
}
\]

The last term is unavoidable.

Thus a positive/increasing weight does not create free additional viscosity. The complete ledger includes

\[
D_tw=\partial_tw+u\cdot\nabla w
\]

and

\[
\Delta w.
\]

## 3. Consequence for `w(|grad p|)`

If

\[
w=F(|\nabla p|),
\]

then `D_t w` and `Delta w` contain derivatives such as

\[
D_t\nabla p,\qquad \nabla^2p,\qquad \nabla^3p,
\]

as well as quadratic Hessian terms generated by the chain rule.

The pressure is nonlocal and satisfies

\[
-\Delta p=\partial_i u_j\,\partial_j u_i
\]

(up to the forcing convention). This Poisson relation gives estimates but no automatic favorable sign for

\[
D_tw+\nu\Delta w.
\]

Therefore the statement

\[
\text{large }|\nabla p|\ \Rightarrow\ \text{stronger weighted dissipation}
\]

is not an identity of the classical NSE unless the weight is proved to satisfy an additional differential inequality that controls this remainder.
