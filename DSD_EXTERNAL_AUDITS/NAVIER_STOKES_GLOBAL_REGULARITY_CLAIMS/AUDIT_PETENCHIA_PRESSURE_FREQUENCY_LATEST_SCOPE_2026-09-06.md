## Exact model scope

The later paper explicitly introduces

\[
\mu_{\rm eff}(p,\Omega)
=\mu_0+\alpha_\mu[f_p(p)-f_\Omega(\Omega)],
\]

an internal field satisfying a constitutive relation of the form

\[
\nabla\Omega
=-\kappa\frac{\nabla p}{|\nabla p|^2+\varepsilon},
\]

and additional symmetric/antisymmetric stresses

\[
\sigma^{(\Omega)},\qquad \sigma^{[\Omega]}.
\]

Thus the momentum equation is an **extended constitutive system**, not merely a weighted test of the original classical NSE.

The additional enstrophy coercivity is correspondingly generated by the additional constitutive terms.

---

## Vanishing-regularization theorem is conditional on the missing Clay-level bound

The paper's vanishing-parameter theorem considers solutions \(u^n\) of the extended systems with

\[
(\alpha_\mu^n,\chi^n,\eta^n)\to0.
\]

To obtain a smooth classical NSE limit it assumes, for every finite \(T\), an \(n\)-independent bound

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^T\|\omega^n(t)\|_{L^\infty}\,dt\le C(T).
}
\]

The paper itself identifies this uniform BKM estimate as the **remaining analytic obstacle** and describes its persistence as conjectural.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\text{fixed-parameter extended-system smoothness}
+\text{conditional vanishing theorem}
\neq
\text{unconditional classical NSE regularity}.
}
\]

The uniform BKM estimate is precisely the kind of continuation control whose derivation is central to the original problem.

---

## DSD interpretation

This is a useful example of correct scope separation:

1. modified system acquires extra anisotropic coercivity;
2. fixed regularization parameters may yield stronger estimates;
3. the classical limit requires a **uniform** estimate that does not degenerate with those parameters;
4. that uniformity is not supplied automatically by convergence of the equations.

Hence the latest paper is better classified as a **regularization research program** rather than a failed classical-NSE theorem.

---

## Relation to the earlier pressure-weighted claim

Earlier descriptions that a positive pressure-gradient weight alone creates stronger classical NSE dissipation must be read with caution. For a genuinely state-dependent weight \(w\), the weighted enstrophy identity contains the full derivative ledger

\[
\frac12\frac d{dt}\int w|\omega|^2
=
\int w\,\omega\cdot S\omega
-\nu\int w|\nabla\omega|^2
+\frac12\int (D_tw+\nu\Delta w)|\omega|^2.
\]

For \(w=w(|\nabla p|)\), the final term contains pressure time/space derivatives and has no automatic favorable sign.

The later extended-system paper avoids pretending this extra coercivity is free: it explicitly inserts constitutive tensors that generate it.
