Date: 2026-08-29

---

## 1. Audit target

M5-194C reprioritized the endpoint from shell commutators to the bulk critical operator.

The present audit tests the strongest remaining scalar structural shortcut:

> Can the scalar potential generated by weighted divergence-free transport combine with the common-tail strain so that the resulting bulk quadratic form is pointwise nonnegative for every admissible critical tail?

The answer is **no**.

The obstruction follows from the trace alone.

---

## 2. Symmetric strain is trace free

Let the critical common tail be divergence free,

\[
\nabla\cdot B_T=0,
\]

and define its symmetric strain matrix

\[
S_{B_T}:=
\frac12\left(\nabla B_T+(\nabla B_T)^T\right).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}S_{B_T}
=\nabla\cdot B_T
=0.
}
\]

At the critical scale,

\[
S_{B_T}=r^{-2}\Sigma_T(y,\theta)
\]

for a dimensionless symmetric trace-free matrix `Sigma_T`.

---

## 3. Scalar weighting can only shift by a multiple of the identity

Let the scalar Carleman weight be `varphi=e^Psi`.

After weighted integration by parts, the divergence-free transport contributes a scalar zeroth-order term proportional to

\[
c_\Psi(x)|W|^2,
\]

where, up to the overall sign convention used to place terms on the two sides of the vorticity equation,

\[
c_\Psi=-B_T\cdot\nabla\Psi.
\]

Thus at the quadratic-form level the transport contribution is a scalar matrix

\[
c_\Psi I.
\]

The strain contribution is symmetric and trace free. Hence the combined critical matrix has the generic form

\[
\boxed{
M_\Psi
=c_\Psi I\;\pm\;S_{B_T}.
}
\]

The sign in front of the strain depends on the operator convention, but the trace argument below does not.

Because `tr S_BT=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{tr}M_\Psi=3c_\Psi.
}
\]

---

## 4. Exact streamline adaptation cannot create positive bulk coercivity

Suppose the scalar phase is exactly adapted:

\[
B_T\cdot\nabla\Psi=0.
\]

Then

\[
c_\Psi=0
\]

and

\[
M_\Psi=\pm S_{B_T}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\operatorname{tr}M_\Psi=0.
\]

A symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix with zero trace must vanish identically, because all of its eigenvalues are nonnegative and their sum is zero.

Hence

\[
M_\Psi\ge0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M_\Psi=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
S_{B_T}=0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
B_T\cdot\nabla\Psi=0
\text{ and }
S_{B_T}\ne0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the transport--strain form is not pointwise nonnegative.}
}
\]

Exact scalar adaptation removes the transport potential but cannot neutralize a nonzero trace-free strain by positivity.

M5-191 already supplies a critical divergence-free rotational test tail with nonzero signed strain, so this is a live obstruction within the audited geometry class.

---

## 5. Radial phase: zero flux forces a bad-sign region

For the radial logarithmic phase used in M5-194A,

\[
\Psi(y)=\beta y,
\qquad y=-\log r,
\]

we have

\[
c_\Psi
\propto
\beta r^{-2}\Phi_r(y,\theta),
\]

again up to the fixed global sign convention.

The divergence-free common tail satisfies zero spherical radial flux,

\[
\int_{S^2}\Phi_r(y,\theta)\,dS=0.
\]

If `Phi_r` is continuous and not identically zero, it must take both positive and negative values on the sphere.

At every point where the chosen sign of `c_Psi` is negative,

\[
\operatorname{tr}M_\Psi=3c_\Psi<0.
\]

A positive-semidefinite matrix cannot have negative trace.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_r\not\equiv0,
\quad
\int_{S^2}\Phi_r=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
M_\Psi\not\ge0
\text{ globally for a radial scalar phase.}
}
\]

Changing the overall orientation of the scalar weight merely interchanges the positive and negative angular sectors; it does not remove the obstruction.

---

## 6. Purely tangential tail does not rescue universal positivity

If

\[
\Phi_r=0,
\]

then the radial weighted-transport scalar vanishes:

\[
c_\Psi=0.
\]

The combined matrix again reduces to

\[
M_\Psi=\pm S_{B_T}.
\]

Since it is trace free, it can be positive semidefinite only if

\[
S_{B_T}=0.
\]

Thus a purely tangential critical tail is favorable for transport adaptation but is not sufficient when the tail carries nonzero strain.

This separates two conditions that must not be conflated:

\[
\boxed{
\Phi_r=0
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
S_{B_T}=0.
}
\]

---

## 7. What has actually been closed

The audit closes the following universal proof mechanism:

\[
\boxed{
\text{scalar weight}
+\text{divergence-free transport}
+\text{strain}
\Longrightarrow
\text{pointwise nonnegative critical bulk form by structural cancellation alone}.
}
\]

The trace obstruction shows that this cannot hold for the full audited critical-tail class.

This is stronger than merely observing that the strain has an indefinite example: it shows why a scalar transport shift cannot universally repair it under exact adaptation or a radial zero-flux geometry.

---

## 8. What remains open

This no-go result does **not** prove that the critical terms cannot be controlled by a Carleman estimate.

Carleman coercivity need not come from positivity of the lower-order transport--strain block itself. The negative part may still be bounded in absolute value and absorbed by positive terms generated by the conjugated diffusion operator.

Therefore the surviving scalar route is now quantitatively narrower:

\[
\boxed{
\text{not cancellation, but coercive domination.}
}
\]

One must compare the normalized negative part of

\[
c_\Psi I\pm S_{B_T}
\]

against the exact positive bulk margin produced by conjugating `partial_t-Delta`.

---

## 9. DSD verdict

### CLOSED

- exact scalar streamline adaptation as a universal source of bulk positivity when `S_BT != 0`;
- radial scalar weighting plus zero spherical flux as a universal positive transport--strain cancellation;
- the inference `Phi_r=0 => harmless bulk tail` without also controlling strain.

### SURVIVES

- absolute-value absorption by a sufficiently strong endpoint Carleman coercivity margin;
- PDE-specific rigidity forcing both small radial component and small/structured strain;
- matrix or vector symmetrizers that are not restricted to a scalar multiple of the identity;
- a different nonradial phase whose transport scalar is nonnegative and quantitatively dominates strain, provided its global characteristic geometry can actually exist;
- backward-uniqueness architectures not based on this pointwise bulk positivity.
