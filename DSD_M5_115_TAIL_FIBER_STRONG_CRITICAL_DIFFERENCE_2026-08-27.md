## 3. Relative Leray equation

Write

\[
V=W+Z.
\]

Subtract the two Leray equations.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\partial_s Z
-\nu\Delta Z
+\frac12Y\cdot\nabla Z
+\frac12Z
+(W\cdot\nabla)Z
+(Z\cdot\nabla)W
+(Z\cdot\nabla)Z
+\nabla\pi
=0,
}
\]

with

\[
\nabla\cdot Z=0.
\]

Equivalently, using the common quotient background `B_T`, the two quotient equations have the same tail forcing, so their difference is an **unforced relative equation**. The tail forcing cancels exactly; only the common background remains in the coefficients.

Thus fiber nonuniqueness is not caused by two different external tail forcings.

## 4. Relative L2 ledger

Because `Z in L2` and the W1 fields are smooth, the relative energy identity is legitimate:

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\frac d{ds}\|Z\|_2^2
+\nu\|\nabla Z\|_2^2
-\frac14\|Z\|_2^2
=-\int Z^T S_W Z\,dY,
}
\]

where

\[
S_W=\frac12(\nabla W+\nabla W^T).
\]

The advective terms generated by divergence-free `W` and `Z` cancel in the ordinary energy pairing.

This identity is not monotone: the Leray linear term has the same `-1/4 ||Z||_2^2` structure already audited for one W1 state, and the background strain can have either sign.

Therefore this ledger alone does not make a fiber collapse.

## 5. Same tail implies the same cubic residue

Let

\[
p=3+\varepsilon.
\]

The compact W1 class has a uniform weak-L3 ceiling and a uniform `L^{3+delta}` bound for every fixed `delta>0`.

Because `Z in L3` and also `Z in L^{3+delta}`, interpolation gives

\[
\sup_{0<\varepsilon<\delta}
\|Z\|_{3+\varepsilon}<\infty.
\]

For `p>3`,

\[
\big||V|^p-|W|^p\big|
\le
p|Z|\left(|V|^{p-1}+|W|^{p-1}\right).
\]

Hence by Holder,

\[
|M_p(V)-M_p(W)|
\le
p\|Z\|_p
\left(
\|V\|_p^{p-1}+\|W\|_p^{p-1}
\right).
\]

The uniform weak-L3 height bound gives the standard near-critical estimate

\[
M_{3+\varepsilon}(V)+M_{3+\varepsilon}(W)
\le\frac{C}{\varepsilon}

for small `epsilon`.

Therefore

\[
\varepsilon|M_{3+\varepsilon}(V)-M_{3+\varepsilon}(W)|
\le
C\varepsilon^{1/(3+\varepsilon)}
\|Z\|_{3+\varepsilon}
\to0.
\]

Thus the Abel/Mellin cubic residue is identical on each tail fiber:

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr R_3(V)=\mathscr R_3(W).
}

Equivalently,

\[
\boxed{

\text{ factors through }\mathfrak T:M\to\mathcal T.

The critical anomaly lives on the tail factor, not in the strong-L3 fiber difference.

\[
\boxed{
\|z(t)\|_2^2

The same-tail fiber quotients have a uniform L2 bound on the compact W1 set, so

\[
\boxed{

\[

The fiber can therefore concentrate a strong-critical packet into the singular center while its physical energy vanishes.

## 7. Why ordinary forward uniqueness is not enough

For each `t<T_*`, the physical solutions are smooth and ordinary forward uniqueness holds from exact data at that earlier time.

But we are asking the reverse question:

\[
\boxed{
\text{can two distinct smooth preterminal histories converge to the same terminal L2 trace while retaining a critical core difference?}
}
\]

Forward uniqueness does not answer this.

The issue is a backward-uniqueness/unique-continuation problem at a Type-I critical terminal concentration.

## 8. Literature boundary

Lei--Yang--Yuan, `Backward Uniqueness for 3D Navier-Stokes Equations With Non-Trivial Final Data and Applications`, International Mathematics Research Notices 2024, prove backward uniqueness for **bounded mild solutions** of 3D Navier--Stokes in the whole space with nontrivial final data.

Taniuchi, `On uniqueness of mild L^{3,infinity}-solutions on the whole time axis to the Navier-Stokes equations in unbounded domains`, Mathematische Annalen 389 (2024), develops uniqueness in weak-L3 classes under smallness/precompactness or specific spatial-decay assumptions.

The present fiber difference does not automatically fall under either result:

- the individual physical W1 realizations have Type-I `tau^-1/2` core growth and are not uniformly bounded up to `T_*`;
- the common background is in the large weak-L3 regime, not a small weak-L3 regime;
- the difference `z` is not itself an autonomous Navier-Stokes solution; it solves a relative parabolic equation with the two W1 solutions as coefficients.

Therefore no published backward-uniqueness theorem is invoked as a closure here.
