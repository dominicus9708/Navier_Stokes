## 1. Purpose

M5-424 leaves a near-balanced branch

\[
C_{bal}^{fresh}
\]

in which old source function age-dilutes and a positive-frequency sequence of fresh efficient natural sources supplies the first-hitting stretching.

A tempting next move is

\[

\Rightarrow

\]

That implication is false without a state-separation hypothesis.

This note separates the two descriptors.

---

## 2. Material freshness

Let `B_j` denote a formed natural companion source carrier at stage `j`.

Call the stage-`j+1` source **materially fresh relative to `B_j`** when a fixed fraction of its current carrier volume/flux is carried by material labels that are not descendants of the retained old source population.

This is a statement about the flow map and label ancestry.

It does not specify how different the Eulerian fields are.

---

## 3. Critical-state novelty

In the stage-`j` natural frame write schematically

\[
\mathcal G_j
=
(\Omega_j,U_j,\Sigma_j;\text{main/source geometry})
\]

modulo the already allowed translation/Galilean normalization and the fixed first-hitting scale normalization.

A new stage has **critical-state novelty** only if its normalized source-bearing state cannot be represented, within a fixed small critical/local compactness tolerance, by the previously retained state family after the allowed frame identifications.

The exact topology can be chosen according to the downstream theorem, for example:

- local strong vorticity convergence on fixed normalized balls;
- local velocity compactness plus shell control;
- or phase-space separation of the `dot H^{-1/2}` carrier probes of M5-408.

The essential point is

\[
\boxed{
\text{material label identity}
\text{ is not part of the Eulerian critical norm.}
}
\]

## 4. Exact anti-shortcut example

Consider any smooth Eulerian pattern that is transported through a region while maintaining approximately the same local velocity/vorticity state in a suitable moving frame.

Different material particles may enter and leave that region even if

\[
U_{j+1}\approx U_j,
\qquad
\Omega_{j+1}\approx\Omega_j
\]

in the normalized Eulerian frame.

The `dot H^{-1/2}` pairing used in M5-408 depends on the vorticity field, not on particle labels.

Therefore a purely material replacement can occur with no new phase-space-orthogonal critical atom.

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{fresh material labels}
\not\Longrightarrow
\text{Bessel novelty}.
}
\]

## 5. Why this matters for the fresh-source branch

M5-395--397 show that generic target-volume replacement can be upgraded to a fixed `O(nu)` directed source flux and has finite coherent storage memory.

Those statements are correct material/flux genealogy statements.

But repeated sequential handoff can in principle occur through a nearly identical normalized Eulerian source state.

Thus a positive generation density of material handoff is not itself a non-summable critical-state count.

Any future nonreuse theorem must charge **state change**, **phase-space separation**, or an actual flux/diffusion/interface action — not the label change alone.

## 6. Label-only handoff with recurrent Eulerian state

Now suppose material source labels are replaced infinitely often, but after first-hitting normalization the source-bearing Eulerian states remain precompact/recurrent on every fixed normalized cylinder and the shell/remote critical controls required by the existing compactness bridge remain bounded.

Then material ancestry is irrelevant to the local compactness limit.

The same route used in M5-398 applies:

1. first-hitting center/time nesting gives an expanding backward tower;
2. local analyticity gives smooth compactness on fixed cylinders;
3. recurrent/compact normalized states generate a nontrivial complete ancient orbit;
4. the ancient orbit enters the `W1/W2` dichotomy.

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{label-fresh but Eulerian-recurrent}
\Longrightarrow
W_1\lor W_2.
}
\]
